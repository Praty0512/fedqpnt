\title{FedQPNT: Federated, Quantum-Sensor-Augmented Positioning, Navigation and Timing with Continuous Attack-Adaptive Trust Fusion}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Author names withheld for review}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Affiliation withheld for review}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) spoofing and jamming threaten autonomous vehicles and unmanned aerial vehicles. We present FedQPNT, which fuses an inertial measurement unit, GNSS and a cold-atom accelerometer (CAI) in an error-state Kalman filter. A learned detector drives a continuous, asymmetric trust law with evidence-bounded exclusion, and detector parameters are aggregated across a fleet by a robust federated-learning (FL) aggregator. The CAI noise model is calibrated to real laboratory data; platform, GNSS observables and attacks are simulated. We report a pre-registered evaluation on 30 unseen seeds per scenario at two inertial grades, with a Holm-corrected confirmatory family of eight tests. FedQPNT detects attack onset significantly earlier than a detect-and-exclude baseline (median paired difference 0.5\,s, Holm-adjusted $p=0.004$); the accompanying position-error test was not significant. It adds no measurable cost in nominal operation or under jamming. Against a displaced meaconer it lowers attack-window horizontal error relative to the baseline at both grades and to the undefended filter at the MEMS grade, and it cuts median clock error about eight-fold (634 versus 5220\,ns). Trust cycling stayed at or below 29 cycles per hour (proven bound: 223), and a four-hour mission showed no drift. The evaluation also characterises where GNSS exclusion does not pay: for small-offset spoofs an inertial coast drifts further than the spoof displaces, and the re-admission dwell dominates post-attack error. The CAI and federated learning showed no measurable effect in this configuration (hypotheses H2--H4 not supported).
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Federated learning, GNSS spoofing, cold-atom interferometry, quantum inertial sensing, sensor fusion, trust-aware fault tolerance, positioning navigation and timing (PNT).
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Motivation}
Positioning, navigation and timing (PNT) for autonomous vehicles and UAVs depends heavily on GNSS, a
signal that is inexpensive to spoof or jam and whose loss or manipulation directly threatens safety.
A wide literature addresses parts of this problem: federated learning has been used to detect GNSS
spoofing across a fleet without centralizing raw sensor data \cite{khan2025enhancing,chai2025navigation,liu2026g2fl,namagembe2026machine,zhou2023spatiotemporal};
classical and learned adaptive filters re-weight or exclude GNSS when it disagrees with inertial
propagation \cite{mehra1970identification,carlson1988federated,carlson1990federated,ren2020adaptive,pardhasaradhi2022gps,negru2024resilient};
and cold-atom interferometers (CAIs) have been proposed and increasingly demonstrated as inertial
sensors whose bias is orders of magnitude below that of classical accelerometers
\cite{wright2022cold,darmagnac2024atom}. Separately, quantum-enhanced and quantum federated learning
have been studied as a way to speed up or secure the \emph{learning computation} itself
\cite{hazarika2025quantum,chehimi2023foundations,chehimi2022quantum}. To date, however, these threads
have not been combined: no verified prior work couples (a) a physical quantum inertial sensor, (b)
fleet-wide federated learning of an attack detector, and (c) a continuous, attack-adaptive trust law
that keeps this detector's output as a smoothly varying confidence rather than a switch. The closest
robust-FL system for GNSS spoofing, G\textsuperscript{2}FL \cite{liu2026g2fl}, addresses robust
aggregation under Byzantine clients but does not couple its detector to a continuously varying,
per-sensor trust weight, nor does it use a physical quantum sensor.

\subsection{Gap statement}
\label{sec:gap}
No verified reference in our bibliography (Section~\ref{sec:related}, Table~\ref{tab:comparison})
implements federated learning together with a physical quantum inertial sensor, and among the FL-based
GNSS-defense papers we could verify, all use a binary detect-and-exclude action rather than a
continuously varying trust weight. Conversely, the continuous/adaptive-trust literature we verified
either has no federated learning component (it is single-node) or, when described as "federated", uses
the classical multi-sensor "federated filter" sense of the term (Carlson-style master/local filter
fusion within one platform) rather than federated learning across distributed clients
\cite{negru2024resilient} -- a distinction we make precise in Section~\ref{sec:related} because the
term is a recurring source of confusion in this literature. The specific, scoped gap this paper
addresses is therefore: \emph{a closed loop in which an FL-trained, fleet-shared spoofing/jamming
detector drives a continuous, evidence-bounded, per-sensor trust weight that gates a quantum-aided
navigation filter, with the quantum sensor's own noise and fault behaviour calibrated to real
laboratory data rather than assumed.}

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item A system architecture in which a single 15-state loosely-coupled error-state Kalman filter is
  shared, unmodified, across every baseline and ablation, so that comparisons isolate the contribution
  of each mechanism (FL, continuous trust, quantum aiding) rather than confounding it with
  implementation differences (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item A continuous, asymmetric trust law with evidence-bounded exclusion (a bounded distrust time, a
  shadow probe that tests returning GNSS against the coasting state without applying it, and separate
  position and clock trust weights) that couples a learned, federated detector to the GNSS, clock and
  quantum measurement covariances via a bounded inflation $R_{\mathrm{eff}} = R / \max(w, w_{\min})$,
  with trust cycling bounded at $223$ cycles per hour independently of the timing of the evidence
  (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item TRIM-NB-R, a trust-aware robust federated aggregator that combines norm clipping, coordinate-wise
  trimmed-mean/median aggregation, and a cosine-similarity reputation score with quarantine, specified
  against a FedAvg reference (FedProx is implemented but not evaluated, as with the frozen $\mu=0$ it coincides with FedAvg) under sign-flip, Gaussian, label-flip and "A Little Is Enough"
  poisoning (Section~\ref{sec:method}); the round-3 evaluation does not include an AUC-based poisoning comparison (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item A quantum inertial sensor model whose noise density, per-interrogation-time noise scaling, and
  bursty fault process are calibrated from real cold-atom interferometer shot data
  \cite{darmagnac2024atom} rather than from published summary statistics alone, together with an
  explicit, honestly reported operating limit (rigid-mode contrast loss above
  $\Omega_c \approx 17\,\mathrm{mrad/s}$) that makes quantum aiding intermittent during ordinary vehicle
  manoeuvres (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
\item A pre-registered, mechanism-class baseline taxonomy (A: FL detect-and-exclude; B-bin: single-node
  detect-and-switch; B-cont: single-node continuous trust; B$'$: classical innovation-based adaptive
  filter) and four falsifiable hypotheses H1--H4, reported whatever their outcome
  (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Paper organisation}
Section~\ref{sec:related} surveys related work in five groups and positions FedQPNT against it.
Section~\ref{sec:threat} specifies the node and threat model. Section~\ref{sec:method} gives the
fusion, trust, and federation equations. Section~\ref{sec:setup} describes the experimental protocol.
Section~\ref{sec:results} and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} report results, discussion and limitations. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes, followed by a reproducibility statement.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Federated learning for GNSS security and spoofing detection}
Several recent papers apply federated learning to GNSS spoofing or jamming detection so that a fleet of
vehicles can share a detector without centralizing raw trajectory data. Khan et al.
\cite{khan2025enhancing} have each vehicle localize itself from dead-reckoning-style signals (yaw
rate, steering angle, wheel speed), compare this against GNSS, and use the resulting localization
mismatch to compute weights that are aggregated at a roadside unit, where a global support-vector
machine classifier is trained by FL; they report 99\% accuracy on CARLA-simulated trajectories. Chai et
al. \cite{chai2025navigation} similarly use federated learning to identify GNSS spoofing and jamming
signals for UAVs. Namagembe et al. \cite{namagembe2026machine} propose a machine-learning-based GPS
spoofing detection and mitigation scheme for UAVs. Zhou et al. \cite{zhou2023spatiotemporal} propose a
spatial-temporal federated transfer learning architecture with multi-sensor data fusion for cooperative
positioning across vehicles; this targets positioning accuracy under heterogeneous data distributions
rather than adversarial spoofing specifically, but it establishes that federated transfer learning is a
workable mechanism for multi-vehicle positioning problems. Closest to our own robust-aggregation
component is G\textsuperscript{2}FL \cite{liu2026g2fl}, which proposes a robust federated learning
scheme explicitly for GNSS spoofing detection under Byzantine or poisoned clients. In every one of these
papers, the FL-trained detector's output drives a \emph{binary} decision at the node
(classify-and-exclude, or classify-and-flag); none couples the detector to a continuously varying
trust weight on the fusion filter's measurement covariance, and none uses a physical quantum sensor.
TRIM-NB-R differs from G\textsuperscript{2}FL's robustness mechanism in aggregation detail (per-round
cosine-similarity reputation with quarantine, rather than G\textsuperscript{2}FL's own scheme), but the
central point of departure from this entire group is architectural: in FedQPNT the FL-trained detector
is one input to a continuous trust law, not the exclude decision itself.

\subsection{Adaptive and fault-tolerant sensor fusion and trust}
The idea of re-weighting or excluding a sensor based on its own innovation statistics predates federated
learning by decades. Mehra \cite{mehra1970identification} introduced innovation-based adaptive
estimation of measurement and process noise for Kalman filters, the root of the "adaptive Kalman filter"
family and the basis of our B$'$ baseline. Carlson's federated filter
\cite{carlson1988federated,carlson1990federated} decomposes a navigation filter into local filters per
sensor feeding a master filter, giving fault tolerance and decorrelation properties; this is the
classical, single-platform sense of "federated," and it is worth stating precisely because the term
recurs, confusingly, in more recent papers. Ren et al. \cite{ren2020adaptive} demonstrate adaptive
sensor fusion of camera, GNSS and IMU for autonomous driving, adjusting fusion weights as sensor quality
changes; we use this as our representative of the single-node, continuous-weighting mechanism class
(B-cont in our taxonomy) because, unlike \cite{mehra1970identification}, it targets exactly the
camera/GNSS/IMU setting our own filter generalizes. Pardhasaradhi and Cenkeramaddi
\cite{pardhasaradhi2022gps} detect GPS spoofing on a drone via distributed radar tracking and, once
spoofing is flagged, switch control to the independent radar track; this is a detect-then-switch
mechanism against an independent second sensor, which we use as the representative of our B-bin
(detect-and-switch) baseline class, while noting that it switches to a different physical sensor
(radar) rather than continuously re-weighting the same GNSS/INS filter. Negru et al.
\cite{negru2024resilient} integrate GNSS, IMU, a monocular camera and a barometer using local
extended Kalman filters feeding a gated-recurrent-unit master filter; despite being described in its own
title as a "hybrid federated fusion architecture," this is the classical Carlson-style federated
\emph{filter} architecture with one learned component inside a single UAV, not federated learning across
distributed clients, and we cite it here, in the fusion-architecture group, rather than alongside the
FL-for-spoofing-detection papers of Section~2.1 -- a reframing that sharpens the gap statement of
Section~\ref{sec:gap}. A companion paper from the same group \cite{negru2024uwb} studies UWB-aided
hybrid navigation in degraded GNSS environments. None of this group's continuous or adaptive fusion
mechanisms is trained or shared across a fleet by federated learning, and none uses a physical quantum
sensor as an independent aiding source.

\subsection{Quantum inertial sensing for navigation}
Cold-atom interferometers measure acceleration or rotation via atom-interferometric phase, giving an
absolute output whose bias is set by fundamental atomic constants rather than by the drift of a
mechanical or MEMS proof mass. Wright et al. \cite{wright2022cold} review cold-atom inertial sensors for
navigation applications, covering the physical principle, achievable sensitivities, and the practical
challenges (dead time, dynamic range, size/weight/power) of fielding such a sensor on a moving platform.
d'Armagnac de Castanet et al. \cite{darmagnac2024atom} report a three-axis hybrid cold-\textsuperscript{87}Rb
atom interferometer operated at arbitrary orientations and rotation rates, providing, to our knowledge,
the most directly usable real dataset for calibrating a moving-platform CAI noise and fault model: per-shot
Raman population-ratio residuals at several interrogation times, and a measured contrast-versus-rotation-rate
law in both a rigid-mirror and an actively tip-tilt-compensated ("inertial-pointing") mode. We use this
dataset directly (Section~\ref{sec:threat}, and D-014/D-015/D-019/D-020/D-021 in the project decision
log) to calibrate our simulated CAI's noise density, its per-interrogation-time scaling, and a
Gilbert--Elliott bursty-outlier channel, rather than assuming these from the summary numbers in the
paper's text alone. Neither \cite{wright2022cold} nor \cite{darmagnac2024atom} addresses fleet-wide
learning or GNSS attack robustness; they are the physical-sensing foundation our system builds on, not a
navigation-security contribution in themselves.

\subsection{Quantum federated learning (distinguished from quantum sensing)}
A separate line of work applies quantum computation to the federated learning process itself, and it is
important to distinguish it from quantum \emph{sensing}, since the two are sometimes conflated. Hazarika
et al. \cite{hazarika2025quantum} propose quantum-enhanced federated learning for metaverse-empowered
vehicular networks, using quantum-enhanced computation to accelerate or improve FL convergence, not a
physical quantum sensor providing navigation-grade measurements. Chehimi et al.
\cite{chehimi2023foundations} lay foundational architectures for quantum federated learning over
classical and quantum communication networks, and a related paper by the same first two authors
\cite{chehimi2022quantum} studies quantum federated learning specifically with quantum \emph{data}
(i.e., data that is itself a quantum state, transmitted over a quantum network) -- again, a statement
about the learning substrate and communication channel, not about inertial or positioning sensors. None
of this group's methods involves a physical quantum sensor of the kind used in FedQPNT (a cold-atom
accelerometer providing a classical-unit acceleration measurement), and none targets GNSS spoofing or
jamming. We cite this group specifically to pre-empt a natural but mistaken reading of "quantum
federated learning for navigation" as prior work equivalent to ours: it is not, since it addresses the
learning computation rather than the sensing modality.

\subsection{GNSS spoofing and jamming signatures}
Detecting GNSS attacks depends on the observable signatures those attacks leave, at the level of raw
receiver measurements rather than final position. Psiaki and Humphreys \cite{psiaki2016gnss} give a
foundational survey of GNSS spoofing mechanisms and detection principles, including the
capture-and-carry-off dynamics that a power-advantage spoofer must use to smoothly pull a receiver's
tracking loops away from the true signal, and the vulnerability of a receiver at the moment of
re-acquisition after an outage. An earlier study by Humphreys et al.
\cite{humphreys2008assessing} assesses the practical spoofing threat and its feasibility with
civilian-grade equipment. Radoš et al. \cite{rados2024recent} survey recent advances in jamming and
spoofing detection in GNSS and report field-measured signatures we rely on directly, including the
collapse of cross-satellite carrier-to-noise-density-ratio (C/N0) correlation under single-antenna
spoofing (from $-0.76$ clean to $0.99$ spoofed) -- a signature our attack and detector-feature models
reproduce explicitly, since a single spoofing antenna necessarily transmits all counterfeit signals with
a common-mode power fluctuation. Borhani-Darian et al. \cite{borhanidarian2024detecting} detect GNSS
spoofing with a deep-learning classifier operating on the cross-ambiguity function in the correlation
domain, a complementary, lower (pre-tracking) level of the receiver signal chain than the
raw-observable (pseudorange/Doppler/C-N0/AGC) level at which our own attacks and detector features
operate. Tariq and Ahanger \cite{tariq2026multimodel} propose a multi-model fusion scheme -- Kalman
filtering combined with ensemble machine learning and a transformer-based detector -- for detecting and
mitigating BeiDou spoofing across a decentralized UAV swarm, reporting approximately 99\% detection
accuracy and a greater-than-97\% mission-success rate under active spoofing; we note, following our own
verification, that this fusion is per-model ensembling rather than an explicit decentralized-consensus
mechanism, and that the frequently quoted "97\%" figure in this line of work is mission-success rate,
not detection accuracy. Together, this group establishes the observable-level attack signatures
(C/N0 correlation collapse, AGC and clock-jump behaviour, RAIM blindness to slow drift-off spoofing) that
motivate our own attack simulator and detector feature set (Section~\ref{sec:threat}).

\section{System and Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Node model}
The system is a fleet of $N$ independent nodes (ground vehicles or UAVs), each running the same code
path. Every node is split into two halves connected only by defined sensor-output types: an
\emph{Environment} half (ground truth trajectory, sensor models, GNSS signal generation, and any
attacks) and an \emph{Agent} half (GNSS receiver, fusion filter, trust engine, learnable detector, and
FL client). Only sensor outputs cross from Environment to Agent; ground truth (\texttt{TruthState}), the
per-tick attack label (\texttt{AttackLabel}), and the environment's own private metadata on
\texttt{GnssEpoch} never do, and this boundary is enforced both by construction (the environment strips
private fields before handing an epoch to the receiver) and by an automated static-analysis guard that
scans the Agent-side packages for forbidden imports of truth or label types. A node runs a 100~Hz global
tick; at each tick the IMU and, when scheduled, the quantum sensor and GNSS receiver are stepped, giving
a fixed per-tick sequence (truth $\rightarrow$ sensors $\rightarrow$ optional attack $\rightarrow$
receiver $\rightarrow$ filter propagation/innovations $\rightarrow$ feature extraction $\rightarrow$
detector $\rightarrow$ trust update $\rightarrow$ filter correction $\rightarrow$ FL round
buffering). Nodes exchange only federated-learning model updates with a server, on a fixed round
schedule (Section~\ref{sec:method}); navigation never blocks on the availability of a fresh global
model, so a node that has lost communications keeps navigating and detecting with its last-known
detector.

\subsection{Sensors}
Each node carries three sensor types feeding one shared, loosely-coupled 15-state error-state Kalman
filter (position, velocity, attitude error, accelerometer bias, gyroscope bias): a classical strapdown
IMU (accelerometer plus gyroscope, one of two grades -- MEMS or tactical -- reported separately because
their free-inertial error growth differs by roughly an order of magnitude); a simulated GNSS receiver
that computes a position/velocity/clock fix by weighted least squares with receiver autonomous integrity
monitoring (RAIM) from per-satellite pseudorange, pseudorange-rate, carrier-to-noise-density ratio and
automatic-gain-control (AGC) observables; and a cold-atom accelerometer (CAI) used in a
hybrid-with-classical configuration, where the CAI's absolute, low-bias output is fused as a direct,
dynamics-independent observation of the classical accelerometer's bias (both instruments sense the same
specific force over the same time window, so gravity, attitude and trajectory terms cancel in the
hybridisation residual). The CAI's noise density, per-interrogation-time scaling, and a bursty
outlier/fringe-jump fault process are calibrated from real per-shot atom-interferometer data
\cite{darmagnac2024atom} rather than assumed from published summary statistics: field-grade operation
uses a $2T = 20\,\mathrm{ms}$ interrogation time, a robust per-shot noise of $\sigma_{\mathrm{shot}}
\approx 5.6\,\mu g$ (within 11\% of the value implied by the paper's own stated signal-to-noise ratio),
and a two-state Gilbert--Elliott channel fitted to a measured $9.0\%$ outlier rate with $66\%$ burst
persistence. The CAI keeps reporting \texttt{valid=True} during a burst -- it is silently wrong, exactly
as a real sensor would be -- so the trust engine, not a validity flag, is what must catch a bad quantum
sample (Section~\ref{sec:method}).

\subsection{Attacker capabilities and limits}
The attacker acts only on the GNSS signal, at the raw-observable level (per-satellite pseudorange,
pseudorange rate, C/N0, lock state, AGC/noise floor), never on the IMU, the quantum sensor, or the
inter-node communication channel in the scenarios reported here. Five attack families are modelled: (i)
gradual (``drift-in'') spoofing, in which a single-antenna spoofer captures the receiver's tracking loops
via a brief power-advantage bump and then drags the code and Doppler measurements smoothly toward a
fake trajectory, remaining statistically close to indistinguishable from clean GNSS to a snapshot RAIM
test; (ii) meaconing (record-and-rebroadcast), which appears as a common-mode clock/delay shift across
all satellites with only a small C/N0 rise; (iii) abrupt, non-aligned spoofing, which cannot capture
smoothly and instead forces a momentary loss of lock, a reacquisition delay, and a discontinuous
position jump on re-lock; (iv) continuous-wave and wideband jamming, which raises the receiver noise
floor and reduces the number of locked satellites; and (v) combined jam-then-spoof, in which a denial
phase is followed by a capture attempt at the moment of reacquisition -- the classic window in which a
receiver is most vulnerable to being handed to a spoofer \cite{psiaki2016gnss}. A single-antenna
spoofer's signature (common-mode C/N0 fluctuation across all spoofed satellites, and the loss of the
normal elevation dependence of C/N0) is modelled explicitly, following field-measured correlation
statistics reported by Radoš et al. \cite{rados2024recent}, and is available to any detector operating
at the raw-observable level, though it is not itself exposed as a hand-crafted detector feature by the
attack layer. In the federated-learning threat surface, a fraction $f$ of nodes may be Byzantine,
submitting arbitrary model updates (sign-flipped, Gaussian-noised, label-flipped, or the "A Little Is
Enough" family of small, aggregate-shifting perturbations) rather than honest gradients; the
self-reported sample count on an update is untrusted, since an attacker could claim an arbitrarily large
$n$ to bias a naive weighted average.

\subsection{What never leaves a node}
No raw sensor stream, trajectory, or attack label ever leaves a node. The only object that crosses a node
boundary to the server is a federated \texttt{ModelUpdate}: a detector weight delta (1010 floating-point
parameters, about 4~kB), a coordinate-wise running mean/standard-deviation pair for feature
normalisation, and a small set of scalar training metrics (sample counts, loss, positive-label rate). The
server never receives ground truth, position, velocity, or raw features. Trust-law hyperparameters and
the fusion filter itself are not learned or shared in this version of the system; they are fixed,
identical across every method under comparison, and tuned once, before any test-seed result is examined,
on a disjoint range of tuning seeds (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
%=====================================================================
This section specifies the components as implemented in the reference simulator. Parameters are tagged
by provenance: \emph{measured} (fitted to the real cold-atom data of \cite{darmagnac2024atom}),
\emph{datasheet} (a manufacturer figure), or \emph{assumed} (a design value that is swept or
stress-tested in the evaluation rather than claimed). Section~\ref{sec:trustlaw} (the trust law) is
deliberately a skeleton: the law is being revised and is described only after it is frozen.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Sensor models}
\label{sec:sensors}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
All sensors are driven by one $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$ simulation tick. The IMU runs at $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$,
the GNSS receiver at $1\,\mathrm{Hz}$ by default, and the CAI at the reciprocal of its measured cycle
time. Each sensor consumes the specific force and angular rate of the simulated trajectory with gravity
already removed upstream, so no sensor model recomputes gravity.

\paragraph{Classical IMU.}
The accelerometer and gyroscope of each axis share one error budget in the IEEE Std~952 style
\cite{ieee952}: a per-run constant turn-on bias, a first-order Gauss--Markov (GM1) bias-instability
process, white noise (velocity/angle random walk), per-run constant scale-factor and misalignment errors,
quantisation and saturation. The GM1 standard deviation is set to $\sigma_{\mathrm{GM}} = B/0.664$ for a
datasheet in-run stability $B$ and a chosen correlation time $\tau_c$, so that the process's
Allan-deviation minimum equals $B$ \cite{jurado2019regression}. Two grades are evaluated and always reported
separately (Table~\ref{tab:imu}): an industrial MEMS grade and a tactical grade. A consumer MEMS grade
exists in the code but is not part of the evaluation.

\paragraph{GNSS receiver.}
The constellation is a synthetic Walker-like GPS almanac with a $10^\circ$ elevation mask, so eight to nine
satellites are visible from the reference origin. Per satellite the simulator generates a pseudorange,
a pseudorange rate, a carrier-to-noise-density ratio (C/N0, rising linearly from $38\,\mathrm{dB\,Hz}$ at
$10^\circ$ elevation to $48\,\mathrm{dB\,Hz}$ at zenith; the curve shape is assumed) and an automatic-gain-control
(AGC) reading. The pseudorange error is one elevation- and C/N0-dependent user-equivalent-range-error
budget composed of code thermal noise, a Gauss--Markov ionospheric residual, a tropospheric delay and
multipath \cite{kaplan2017understanding}. The receiver tracks each satellite with a $25\,\mathrm{dB\,Hz}$
lock threshold and a $1\,\mathrm{s}$ reacquisition delay, and solves a position/velocity/clock
\emph{fix} by weighted least squares with weights equal to the inverse of the same error budget
\cite{misra2006global}, followed by a snapshot residual-based receiver autonomous integrity monitoring
(RAIM) $\chi^2$ statistic \cite{pullen2020gnss}. A fix needs at least four locked satellites. It carries the
position and velocity covariances, the clock bias and drift, and the per-satellite C/N0 and elevation that
the detector uses (Section~\ref{sec:detector}).

\paragraph{Cold-atom accelerometer (CAI).}
The CAI is a Mach--Zehnder light-pulse interferometer whose phase is $\phi = k_{\mathrm{eff}}\,a\,T^2$ with
$k_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1.61\times10^{7}\,\mathrm{rad/m}$ for the $^{87}$Rb D$_2$ Raman pair. The fringe is
observed only modulo $2\pi$ (a fringe period of $3.9\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ at $T = 10\,\mathrm{ms}$),
so the model resolves the fringe order with a classical companion accelerometer, as in hybrid
matter-wave/classical sensors \cite{cheiney2018navigation,templier2022tracking,wright2022cold}. The acceleration response is a triangular weighting
over $[t-2T,\,t]$, followed by a dead time to the end of the cycle. The primary (FIELD) grade is anchored to
the real shot data of \cite{darmagnac2024atom,darmagnac2024dataset} through four fitted elements
(Table~\ref{tab:cai}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Noise level.} The per-shot noise is Gaussian with the robust (MAD-based) standard deviation
  measured at $2T = 20\,\mathrm{ms}$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{shot}} = 5.60\,\mu g$ (bootstrap 95\% CI
  $[5.04, 6.40]\,\mu g$, $n = 491$ shots). This equals $6.97\,\mu g/\sqrt{\mathrm{Hz}}$ at the measured
  per-shot cycle time of $1.548\,\mathrm{s}$. Other interrogation times use the measured per-$2T$ values,
  interpolated linearly in $2T$ and explicitly \emph{not} scaled as $1/T^2$, because the measured
  $10\rightarrow 20\,\mathrm{ms}$ improvement is only about $1.9\times$ (a vibration-limited regime).
\item \emph{Bursty outliers.} A per-axis two-state Gilbert--Elliott channel reproduces the measured
  outlier statistics: a stationary bad-state probability of $9.0\%$ (95\% CI $[6.7, 11.8]\%$) and a
  persistence $P(\mathrm{bad}\,|\,\mathrm{bad}) = 65.9\%$, i.e.\ $7.4\times$ more outlier adjacency than
  independence would give. In the bad state a random-signed magnitude drawn from the 44 empirical
  outliers ($\approx 30$--$270\,\mu g$) is added. The shot-noise draws that feed the model are the
  outlier-free ones, so outliers are never double counted.
\item \emph{Contrast loss with rotation.} The contrast is $C = C_0\exp\!\big(-(\Omega_\perp/\Omega_c)^2\big)$,
  where $\Omega_\perp$ is the rms rate about the two body axes orthogonal to the sensed axis over the cycle.
  The rigid-mirror fit gives $C_0 = 0.394 \pm 0.017$ and $\Omega_c = 48.2 \pm 2.5\,\mathrm{mrad/s}$ at
  $2T = 12\,\mathrm{ms}$. Because the Coriolis phase scales as $\Omega\,T^2$, $\Omega_c$ is scaled as
  $1/T^2$ from this single measured point to $17.3\,\mathrm{mrad/s}$ at $T = 10\,\mathrm{ms}$. This scaling
  is physics-derived and unverified away from $T\approx 6\,\mathrm{ms}$.
\item \emph{Cycle time.} $1.548\,\mathrm{s}$ per shot, the interlaced $k_D/k_U$ output interval of the
  instrument.
\end{enumerate}
The sample is flagged invalid when $C < 0.08$ (assumed; about $\Omega_\perp \gtrsim 22\,\mathrm{mrad/s}$
at FIELD) or when $|a|$ exceeds the dynamic range of $2g$ (assumed). The sample is \emph{not} flagged
invalid during an outlier burst: the sensor is silently wrong, as the real instrument would be, and its
reported variance excludes the burst. The classical Thales-MICAL accelerometer record of the same dataset is
never used as a quantum-noise source. A ``lab'' grade (a short-$T$ compact hybrid, not a best-sensitivity
instrument) and a ``near-future'' roadmap grade exist only for sensitivity sweeps and are labelled as such.
The default pointing mode is rigid; an inertial-pointing mode is an assumption-based extrapolation and is
not used for primary results.

\subsection{Fusion filter}
\label{sec:fusion}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
All methods share one loosely coupled 15-state error-state extended Kalman filter (ESKF). The nominal state
is position $p$ and velocity $v$ in a local ENU frame, the attitude matrix $C$, and the accelerometer and
gyroscope biases $b_a, b_g$; the error state is
$\delta x = [\delta p,\ \delta v,\ \psi,\ \delta b_a,\ \delta b_g] \in \mathbb{R}^{15}$, where $\psi$ is the
navigation-frame attitude error \cite{sola2017quaternion}. Strapdown mechanisation uses trapezoidal integration
of bias-corrected specific force and rate with a coning correction. The process model has the standard
error-propagation blocks $F$ and noise-input blocks $G$ and continuous noise
$Q_c = \mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{VRW}^2 I_3,\ \mathrm{ARW}^2 I_3,\ q_{b_a} I_3,\ q_{b_g} I_3)$ read from the IMU's
own configuration, with $q_b = 2\sigma_{\mathrm{GM}}^2/\tau_c$. During IMU dropouts the last sample is held
and $Q_c$ is inflated by a factor of $10$ ($100$ beyond a $0.1\,\mathrm{s}$ gap). The initial covariance is
$P_0 = \mathrm{diag}\big((3\,\mathrm{m})^2, (0.1\,\mathrm{m/s})^2,\ (\sigma_{\mathrm{tilt}},\sigma_{\mathrm{tilt}},35\,\mathrm{mrad})^2,\ \sigma_{b_a,0}^2,\ \sigma_{b_g,0}^2\big)$
with the turn-on bias standard deviations of Table~\ref{tab:imu} and the roll/pitch term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tilt0}.

\paragraph{Initial roll/pitch covariance.}
The filter is initialised static and levelled from the mean specific force. An accelerometer turn-on bias
$\sigma_{b_a,0}$ tilts that levelling by about $\sigma_{b_a,0}/g$ (roughly $10\,\mathrm{mrad}$ for the MEMS grade), so a fixed
$1\,\mathrm{mrad}$ prior claims more than the sensor can deliver. We set
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\mathrm{tilt}} = \max\!\big(1\,\mathrm{mrad},\ \sigma_{b_a,0}/g\big),
\label{eq:tilt0}
\end{equation}
a property of the levelling procedure and not a tuned constant.

\paragraph{Second-order attitude-error term.}
The linear error model $\delta\dot v = [f_n\times]\psi + \dots$ has no vertical coupling to a horizontal tilt: for
$f_n \approx g\hat z$ and horizontal $\psi_h$, $[f_n\times]\psi$ is horizontal. The neglected second-order term of the
velocity error,
\begin{equation}
d_2 = \tfrac12\,\psi\times(\psi\times f_n) = \tfrac12\big(\psi\psi^{\top} - \lVert\psi\rVert^2 I\big) f_n,
\label{eq:so}
\end{equation}
is $\approx -\tfrac12 g\lVert\psi_h\rVert^2\hat z$: a one-signed vertical velocity error that grows with the square of the
tilt. After a long GNSS exclusion this can far exceed the linear filter's vertical-velocity standard deviation, so the coast covariance
is over-confident; the shadow-probe NIS against a returning fix then exceeds its bound, the probe never passes, and GNSS is never re-admitted.
We therefore add the second moment of $d_2$ under $\psi\sim\mathcal N(0,P_{\psi\psi})$ to the covariance propagation.
By Isserlis' theorem $\mathbb E[d_2 d_2^{\top}]$ is a closed-form quadratic form in $P_{\psi\psi}$ and $f_n$; it is taken about zero, so it covers the
mean $-\tfrac12[\mathrm{tr}(P_{\psi\psi})I - P_{\psi\psi}]f_n$ as well as the fluctuation. Let $B_k\in\mathbb R^{15\times3}$ satisfy
$B_kB_k^{\top} = \Delta t^2\,\mathbb E[d_2d_2^{\top}]$, nonzero only in the velocity rows. Because the term is persistent and
one-signed, its contributions add coherently rather than as independent noise, so a tracked block $C_k\in\mathbb R^{15\times3}$ carries
it through the dynamics:
\begin{align}
P_{k+1} &= \Phi P_k\Phi^{\top} + Q_{d} + B_k(\Phi C_k)^{\top} + (\Phi C_k)B_k^{\top} + B_kB_k^{\top},\nonumber\\
C_{k+1} &= \Phi C_k + B_k.
\label{eq:sotrack}
\end{align}
(The $\Phi C_kC_k^{\top}\Phi^{\top}$ part is already inside $\Phi P_k\Phi^{\top}$, because $P_k$ contains $C_kC_k^{\top}$.) The term has
no tuning constant and is part of the shared core, so every method has it.

\paragraph{Bias model.}
The default bias blocks of $F$ are zero, i.e.\ $b_a$ and $b_g$ are random walks. Applying the GM1 decay
$-I/\tau_c$ to the whole bias error state also decays the share of the prior that comes from the
non-decaying per-run turn-on bias; $P$ then shrinks toward the small GM1 stationary variance while the true
error does not, which produced a severe pure-inertial covariance inconsistency (a factor $e^{-2t/\tau}$).
The mean-reverting form is retained only for ablation, and $q_b$ is kept as an over-bound of the GM1 part.

\paragraph{Measurement updates.}
The GNSS fix is processed as one six-dimensional position/velocity update, $\nu_{\mathrm{g}} = [\hat p - p_{\mathrm{g}};\ \hat v - v_{\mathrm{g}}]$,
$H_{\mathrm{g}} = [I_6\ \ 0]$, with a zero lever arm and
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{g}} = \kappa_R\,\mathrm{blkdiag}\big(\Sigma_{p}^{\mathrm{fix}},\ \Sigma_{v}^{\mathrm{fix}}\big),
\label{eq:rgnss}
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma^{\mathrm{fix}}$ are the covariances reported by the receiver (a fixed diagonal fallback is
used when they are not symmetric positive definite). The inflation $\kappa_R$ compensates the
time-correlated error of a loosely coupled fix (ionosphere, troposphere, multipath), which the update
treats as white. It is set once, on tuning seeds, identically for every method, so that the nominal
horizontal ANEES is close to one. It is $\kappa_R = 60$, a single constant that every layer of the code
resolves to and that is frozen with the core; it was re-tuned from an earlier $40$ after the covariance-hygiene
and soft-gating fixes described below changed the propagated covariance. We do not add a
correlated-error state, because a slow drift spoof would be absorbed into it as ``correlated noise''.
The attitude and bias states may remain over-confident at this $\kappa_R$ (against the effective-bias truth,
which includes turn-on bias, GM1, random-walk and scale-factor/misalignment aliasing; an earlier measurement,
before the covariance corrections of this section, found the accelerometer-bias NEES about six times its nominal value on
tuning seeds); this is a stated limitation (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The round-3 campaign measured the position ANEES only (S1: $0.84$ and $0.94$, inside the band $[0.5,2]$) and did not re-measure the attitude and bias states, so the earlier figure is the latest available.

The CAI enters as a direct observation of the classical accelerometer bias. Over the sample's response
window $W$ (length $T_W = 2T$), the filter forms the same-window, triangularly weighted mean of the
bias-corrected IMU specific force, $\bar f_{\mathrm{IMU}}$, and the innovation is
$\nu_{\mathrm{q}} = \bar f_{\mathrm{Q}} - \bar f_{\mathrm{IMU}} \approx \delta b_a + (\text{CAI noise})$,
with $H_{\mathrm{q}} = [0\ 0\ 0\ I\ 0]$ on the sensed axes and
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{q}} = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{\mathrm{shot}}^2) + \frac{\mathrm{VRW}^2}{T_W}\,I + \sigma_{\mathrm{win}}^2 I,
\qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{win}} = 10^{-5}\,g\ \text{(assumed)},
\label{eq:rq}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{\mathrm{win}}$ covers window mismatch and misalignment. Because both instruments sense the
same specific force in the same window, gravity, attitude and trajectory cancel in $\nu_{\mathrm{q}}$. The
update is skipped when the sample is flagged invalid.

\paragraph{Trust coupling and gating.}
For a sensor $s$ with trust weight $w_s \in [w_{\min}, 1]$, trust enters only through the effective
covariance and an exclusion rule,
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{eff},s} = \frac{R_s}{\max(w_s,\,w_{\min})},\qquad \text{skip the update if } w_s < w_{\mathrm{excl}},
\label{eq:reff}
\end{equation}
with $w_{\min} = 0.02$ and $w_{\mathrm{excl}} = 0.05$ (assumed). For the GNSS position/velocity update the
weight is the position weight $w_{\mathrm{pos}}$ of Section~\ref{sec:trustlaw}, and the update is additionally
suspended, not merely down-weighted, while the trust engine is probing GNSS in shadow mode (the innovation is
evaluated but not applied). A $\chi^2$ gate at level
$\alpha_{\mathrm{gate}} = 10^{-4}$ is part of the shared core, so every method except the deliberately
gate-free ``undefended'' reference has it. With $S_{\mathrm{eff}} = H P H^{\top} + R_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and
$\mathrm{NIS}_{\mathrm{eff}} = \nu^{\top} S_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}\nu$, an update whose NIS exceeds
$\chi^2_{m,\,1-\alpha_{\mathrm{gate}}}$ is not dropped but \emph{softly} gated: its covariance is scaled,
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{eff}} \leftarrow R_{\mathrm{eff}}\cdot \frac{\mathrm{NIS}_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\chi^2_{m,\,1-\alpha_{\mathrm{gate}}}},
\label{eq:softgate}
\end{equation}
so its effective NIS equals the gate (a Huber-type covariance-scaling robust update). We adopted this after
diagnosing that a hard gate drops a slowly dragged GNSS fix once it leaves the $\chi^2$ region, after which
inertial coasting diverges; this is a defect of the shared core that affected every gated method, and the
hard gate is kept only as an ablation. Updates use the Joseph form. After each propagation and update the
covariance is symmetrised and only its negative eigenvalues are clipped, $P \leftarrow V\max(\Lambda,0)V^{\top}$,
with no additive floor: an earlier additive $10^{-12} I$ floor injected unmodelled noise of the order of the
real per-step gyro-bias process noise, and replacing it by the eigenvalue clip removed a covariance excess measured in a single-source regression test.

\paragraph{Clock filter.}
Timing is estimated by a standalone two-state Kalman filter (\texttt{ClockKF}) on the clock bias $b$ and
drift $d$ (metres and metres per second, i.e.\ $c$ times the physical quantities) with
$b_{k+1} = b_k + d_k\Delta t$ and a random-walk drift, and process noise
$Q(\Delta t) = \mathrm{diag}(q_b\Delta t,\ q_d\Delta t)$. The receiver oscillator is modelled as a
temperature-compensated crystal oscillator (TCXO): with the two-state clock model of
\cite{brown2012introduction}, whose TCXO $h$-parameters $h_0 = 2\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm{s}$ and
$h_{-2} = 2\times10^{-20}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ are those used in \cite{krawinkel2021improved} (flicker-frequency noise
$h_{-1}$ is not modelled), the spectral densities are $q_b = h_0/2$ and $q_d = 2\pi^2 h_{-2}$
\cite{qin2021benefits}, which in range units (scaled by $c^2$) are
\begin{equation}
q_b = \tfrac{1}{2}c^2 h_0 \approx 9.0\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^2/s},\qquad
q_d = 2\pi^2 c^2 h_{-2} \approx 3.6\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{(m/s)^2/s}.
\label{eq:tcxo}
\end{equation}
The simulated truth clock uses the same values, so the filter is model-consistent by construction. This
replaced an earlier assumed $q_b = 1\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, about two orders of magnitude above a TCXO, and a truth
clock far quieter than a TCXO in drift, which had flattered holdover. The process noise is oscillator physics and
is not scaled by trust. The filter is aided by the fix's clock estimates with
$R_{\mathrm{clk}} = \mathrm{diag}(3^2\,\mathrm{m}^2,\ 0.2^2\,(\mathrm{m/s})^2)/\max(w_{\mathrm{clk}}, w_{\min})$,
the same trust convention as Eq.~\eqref{eq:reff} but driven by the clock weight $w_{\mathrm{clk}}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:trustlaw}. When $w_{\mathrm{clk}} < w_{\mathrm{excl}}$ (and at zero weight while the clock law
is probing) or when there is no valid fix, the update is skipped and the filter holds over on the oscillator
model. The hard holdover is required because, with $R$-inflation alone, the unconditional process noise re-opens
the gain during a sustained low-trust period. The measurement sigmas $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$
are assumed. The same $q_b, q_d$ normalise the clock-jump evidence of Eq.~\eqref{eq:clkjump}.

\subsection{Learned detector}
\label{sec:detector}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Features.}
At each GNSS epoch a causal, stateful extractor produces a $15$-dimensional feature vector from the fix
and the filter's \emph{nominal-$R$} pre-correction innovations. Nominal-$R$ innovations are used so a
feature cannot depend on its own trust output. The features are: (1--2) the position and velocity
normalised innovation squares divided by their degrees of freedom; (3) the RAIM statistic divided by
$\max(n_{\mathrm{sat}}-4, 1)$; (4--5) the mean C/N0 relative to $45\,\mathrm{dB\,Hz}$ and its
cross-satellite standard deviation; (6) the C/N0 rate; (7) the AGC reading; (8--9) the clock-bias and
clock-drift jumps against a constant-drift prediction, in units of the predicted innovation standard deviation
over the time since the last fix with a valid clock estimate, Eq.~\eqref{eq:clkjump} ($3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at
one-second spacing);
(10) the WLS residual rms; (11) the change in the number of satellites; (12) a Page CUSUM of feature~1,
$S_j = \max(0, S_{j-1} + x_{1,j} - 1.5)$; (13) an indicator that the previous epoch was an outage;
(14) the mean pairwise correlation of per-satellite C/N0 over a 20-epoch window; and (15) the slope of C/N0 on
elevation across tracked satellites. Features 14 and 15 encode the single-antenna spoofing signature
(common-mode C/N0 fluctuation and loss of the elevation dependence) reported in field data
\cite{rados2024recent}. Features that remain observable during an outage (C/N0, AGC, satellite count) are
kept then, because they carry the strongest jamming evidence; only the fit-dependent ones are zeroed.
Each raw vector is normalised with running means and standard deviations updated on negative samples only,
and expanded to a $60$-dimensional input
$u_j = [\tilde x_j,\ \mathrm{EWMA}_{2\,\mathrm{s}}(\tilde x)_j,\ \mathrm{EWMA}_{20\,\mathrm{s}}(\tilde x)_j,\ \tilde x_j - \tilde x_{j-1}]$
with time constants in seconds so that the detector does not depend on the GNSS rate.

\paragraph{Architecture and calibration.}
The detector is a multilayer perceptron $60 \rightarrow 16\ (\tanh) \rightarrow 2\ (\mathrm{sigmoid})$ with a
spoofing head and a jamming head ($1010$ parameters, about $4\,\mathrm{kB}$ in \texttt{float32}). A
logistic-regression detector ($60\rightarrow 2$) is the required ablation. Each head's output is Platt
calibrated on the logit, $p = \sigma\!\big(a\,\mathrm{logit}(p_{\mathrm{raw}}) + b\big)$, with $(a,b)$ fitted at
the natural class ratio on held-out tuning missions and applied at runtime, so the trust engine consumes
calibrated probabilities \cite{guo2017calibration}. The detector output is $p = \max(p_{\mathrm{spoof}}, p_{\mathrm{jam}})$.

\paragraph{Supervised training with oracle labels.}
The detector of every learning method is trained on ground-truth-labelled \emph{training missions}, the
same supervision used by the supervised FL detectors we compare against \cite{khan2025enhancing,chai2025navigation}.
Missions are run through the real closed-loop pipeline (real ESKF innovations, receiver and CAI, with a
fixed-trust, non-detecting configuration so the mission is not perturbed by an untrained detector), and the
oracle label is joined to the feature stream by timestamp \emph{offline}, in a single module that lies
outside the agent's import graph (enforced by a static test). At deployment and on every evaluation
mission no label reaches a node. The training mix has six attack families (gradual spoofing, meaconing,
abrupt spoofing, continuous-wave jamming, wideband jamming, and jam-then-spoof), with one mission in ten
clean and the families assigned round-robin by seed. Jamming severities are set through the jammer's
effective isotropic radiated power so that the range spans partial degradation (the fix stays valid)
through full denial; at the default geometry every severity would otherwise saturate at full denial, which
had left the earlier mix with too few jamming positives. The mix targets at least $500$ positive epochs
per family. The loss is a class-weighted binary cross-entropy per head, optimised by stochastic gradient
descent (learning rate $0.05$, batch $64$). Two stabilising rules apply. The positive fraction is capped
at $50\%$ by down-sampling (or, when fewer than ten negatives exist, all samples are used with class
weights, closing a dead zone in which a node with few negatives previously skipped training). The
inverse-frequency class weights $w_c = \min(N/(2N_c),\,10)$ are capped at $10$, to bound the update norm when one
class is a tiny minority of a batch; an honest node's update with a norm $20$--$70$ times its peers' had motivated the cap. A label-free variant, using hindsight pseudo-labels from heuristic physical rules and a
CAI-aided-inertial divergence statistic, was the original design; its measured pseudo-label precision
failed its acceptance gate on tuning missions, so it is retained only as a reported ablation and is not part
of H1--H4.

\paragraph{Retraining after the filter change (v5).}
Features 1, 2 and 12 are computed from the filter's innovations and covariance, so the covariance corrections above change them
(on tuning seeds features 1 and 2 changed in every epoch and feature 12 in about a quarter; all others were bit-identical).
The detector used for the primary results (v5) is therefore retrained with the unchanged protocol of this subsection (seeds 500--549 training,
550--574 Platt calibration, 575--599 held out). The detector loader raises an error if the weight file is missing; an earlier
silent fallback to an untrained detector is removed.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Trust engine}
\label{sec:trustlaw}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
The trust engine turns the calibrated detector probability $p$ and a small set of physical evidence into
continuous per-sensor weights. It produces a \emph{position} weight $w_{\mathrm{pos}}$, which governs the
GNSS position/velocity update of the filter, a \emph{clock} weight $w_{\mathrm{clk}}$, which governs the
\texttt{ClockKF}, a quantum weight $w_{\mathrm{q}}$ and an IMU weight, and reports the alias
$w_{\mathrm{gnss}} = \min(w_{\mathrm{pos}}, w_{\mathrm{clk}})$ for metrics. Trust enters the filter only
through Eq.~\eqref{eq:reff}, with two additions described below (a suspended update while probing, and a
clock holdover). This subsection describes the law as frozen in the code. The law and filter described here are those of git tag \texttt{core-freeze-6}.
The law was revised several times on tuning seeds before the freeze, in response to failures found there
(unbounded time spent excluded, a probe that dragged the state onto a persisting spoof, and
position-domain exclusion driven by a clock-domain signature); each revision is a design iteration on
tuning data, made before any test-seed run, and is disclosed as such.

\paragraph{Smoothing, detection flag and recovery gate.}
Each weight is driven by one instance of the same core recursion, evaluated at every valid GNSS epoch $j$.
The detector probability is smoothed, mapped to a target weight, and turned into a hysteretic detection
flag $D$:
\begin{align}
\bar p_j &= \bar p_{j-1} + \beta_j\,(p_j - \bar p_{j-1}), && \beta_j = 1 - e^{-\Delta t_j/\tau_p},\ \ \tau_p = 0.5\,\mathrm{s}, \label{eq:pbar}\\
w^{\star}_j &= w_{\min} + (1-w_{\min})\,\mathrm{clip}\!\Big(\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{hi}} - \bar p_j}{\theta_{\mathrm{hi}}-\theta_{\mathrm{lo}}},\,0,\,1\Big), && \theta_{\mathrm{lo}} = 0.2,\ \theta_{\mathrm{hi}} = 0.8. \label{eq:target}
\end{align}
$D$ is set when $\bar p \ge \theta_{\mathrm{on}} = 0.6$ has held for $T_{\mathrm{on}} = 0.5\,\mathrm{s}$ and is
cleared when $\bar p \le \theta_{\mathrm{off}} = 0.3$ has held for $T_{\mathrm{off}} = 5\,\mathrm{s}$. The
weight $w$ falls fast and rises slowly,
\begin{equation}
w \leftarrow w + \big(1-e^{-\Delta t/\tau}\big)\,(w^{\star}-w),\qquad
\tau = \begin{cases}\tau_d = 0.5\,\mathrm{s} & w^{\star} < w,\\ \tau_r = 10\,\mathrm{s},\ \text{only if } G & w^{\star} > w,\end{cases}
\qquad w \in [w_{\min}, 1],
\label{eq:ramp}
\end{equation}
where a rise is permitted only when the recovery gate $G$ is open. The gate requires $D = 0$, a clean dwell of
$T_{\mathrm{clean}} = 10\,\mathrm{s}$ since $\bar p$ last exceeded $\theta_{\mathrm{off}}$, and at least $90\%$ of
the epochs in that window with a position innovation $x_1 \le \chi^2_{3,0.95}/3$ (the dof-normalised
nominal-$R$ NIS of Section~\ref{sec:detector}). An anti-lockout rule opens the gate, with the target capped at
$w_{\mathrm{cap}} = 0.5$, if only the NIS-fraction condition has been failing for $T_{\mathrm{lock}} = 120\,\mathrm{s}$
while all normalised features $3$--$11$ stay within $\pm 1.96$. For the GNSS weights, the first fix that follows an
outage of more than $T_{\mathrm{gap}} = 5\,\mathrm{s}$ has its weight capped at $w_{\mathrm{reacq}} = 0.5$ unless
the consistency test below waives the cap; $w_{\min} = 0.02$ and $w_{\mathrm{excl}} = 0.05$ are as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:reff}.

\paragraph{Bounded exclusion: TRUST, DISTRUST and PROBE.}
The recursion above alone has no bound on the time spent excluded: a chronically mis-firing detector kept
$D=1$ indefinitely, pinned the weight at $w_{\min}$ and let free-inertial error grow without limit on
nominal missions. Each law instance is therefore a three-state machine. It starts in TRUST. When $D$ is
set it enters DISTRUST, where the weight follows Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramp}. After $T_{\mathrm{ex}} = 60\,\mathrm{s}$
in DISTRUST it enters PROBE, the \emph{only} exit from DISTRUST, which lasts $T_{\mathrm{probe}} = 10\,\mathrm{s}$
and whose weight is reported as $w_{\mathrm{probe}} = 0.3$ irrespective of $p$. At the end of the probe the
law returns to TRUST if the probe statistic is within its bound and no physical spoof evidence
routed to that law (Table~\ref{tab:trustsplit}) was observed during the probe, and otherwise returns to DISTRUST for another
$T_{\mathrm{ex}}$. On a successful exit the learned detector is \emph{suppressed} for
$T_{\mathrm{sup}} = 120\,\mathrm{s}$: $p$ is treated as $0$ unless physical evidence fires, which prevents a
mis-firing detector from re-locking the filter into exclusion, while the weight then recovers through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramp}. Physical evidence is never masked by the suppression: whenever it fires, the recursion
is fed $p = 1$. Jamming evidence (AGC drop, C/N0 loss, lost lock) does not block a probe exit, because
jamming degrades rather than deceives and the receiver's own covariance already down-weights a degraded fix;
invalid fixes are simply absent. Because DISTRUST can only be left through PROBE, successive entries into
DISTRUST are separated by at least $T_{\mathrm{ex}} + T_{\mathrm{probe}} = 70\,\mathrm{s}$ by construction.
Consequently the number of trust cycles per hour (a downward crossing of $w = 0.5$ followed by an upward
crossing of $w = 0.9$) is bounded without any assumption on the timing of the evidence: from the ramp time
constant $\tau_{\mathrm{r}}$ and the $0.5$ and $0.9$ crossings, every cycle lasts at least
$L = 16.094\,\mathrm{s}$, so $N_{\mathrm{cyc}} \le \lfloor 3600/16.094 \rfloor = 223$ per hour, and
$N_{\mathrm{cyc}} \le 133$ per hour when fixes arrive on a regular $1\,\mathrm{Hz}$ grid. The bound is
independent of the navigation filter and is exercised by a test suite of periodic, adversarial and random
toggling sequences; the counts observed in the S7 scenarios are in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Physical spoof evidence $E_s$.}
$E_s$ is a set of rule-based signatures that do not depend on the learned detector. It has four members.
(i)~\emph{Position jump.} A short-baseline GNSS-versus-INS test compares the change of the GNSS fix over one
epoch with the change of the filter's own pre-correction estimate over the same epoch,
$d_k = (p_{\mathrm{g},k} - p_{\mathrm{g},k-1}) - (\hat p^{-}_k - \hat p^{-}_{k-1})$, with
$\hat p^{-}_k = p_{\mathrm{g},k} + \nu_{p,k}$ recovered from the nominal-$R$ innovation, and fires when
\begin{equation}
d_k^{\top}\big(\Sigma_{p,k} + \Sigma_{p,k-1} + \sigma_{\mathrm{ins}}^2 I_3\big)^{-1} d_k > \chi^2_{3,\,0.999} \approx 16.27,
\qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{ins}} = 0.05\,\mathrm{m},
\label{eq:jump}
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma_p$ are the fix covariances and $\sigma_{\mathrm{ins}}$ is an assumed one-epoch inertial drift
bound that is independent of the filter covariance. The filter's own NIS is deliberately not used: it
contains the filter's $P$ and therefore cannot separate a spoof from filter self-divergence, and it was
observed to couple the evidence to the trust law's own exclusion dynamics. A slow drift changes little from one
epoch to the next and does not fire the test, an abrupt jump appears entirely in one epoch and does. After a
gap of more than $1.5$ nominal epochs (for example a jamming outage) the baseline is stale by the whole gap, so
it is reset and the position term is suppressed for two epochs, the first fix and the epoch after its
corrective pull. The same two-epoch quarantine of the position term (only; the clock, C/N0 and cross-satellite members
and the detector are not quarantined) applies after a PROBE$\rightarrow$TRUST transition, because the first corrective
update of the coasted state produces a one-epoch jump in the pre-correction estimate that is not spoof evidence. (ii)~\emph{Clock jump} (\texttt{clk\_event}). With $\Delta t$ the time since the last fix with a valid clock estimate (invalid fixes emitted during an outage do not reset it),
\begin{align}
x_8 &= \frac{\lvert b_k - (b_{k-1} + d_{k-1}\Delta t)\rvert}{\sigma_b(\Delta t)}, &
x_9 &= \frac{\lvert d_k - d_{k-1}\rvert}{\sigma_d(\Delta t)}, \nonumber\\
\sigma_b^2(\Delta t) &= \sigma_{b0}^2 + q_b\Delta t + q_d\Delta t^3/3, &
\sigma_d^2(\Delta t) &= \sigma_{d0}^2 + q_d\Delta t, \label{eq:clkjump}
\end{align}
with $\sigma_{b0} = 3\,\mathrm{m}$, $\sigma_{d0} = 0.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the TCXO densities
$q_b, q_d$ of Section~\ref{sec:fusion}. The event fires when $x_8 \ge 5$ or $x_9 \ge 5$. The normalisation by
the \emph{predicted} innovation standard deviation over the gap matters after long outages: with the
oscillator model, the clock innovation standard deviation after a $180\,\mathrm{s}$ outage is about
$263\,\mathrm{m}$, so ordinary oscillator holdover drift is not a spurious jump, but a replay-delay step is
detected only in proportion to its size against this standard deviation (a $750\,\mathrm{m}$ step is about
$2.9\sigma$; see the limitation in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). (iii)~\emph{Cross-satellite C/N0
correlation} (\texttt{xsat\_event}): feature $x_{14}$ exceeds $\mu_{14} + 1.96\,\sigma_{14}$. (iv)~\emph{C/N0
bump} (\texttt{cn0\_event}): feature $x_4$ exceeds $\mu_4 + 1.96\,\sigma_4 + 3\,\mathrm{dB}$, the signature of
a meaconing power advantage. The reference values $\mu, \sigma$ in (iii)--(iv) are the running clean-only
statistics of the detector's normaliser, with $\sigma$ floored at a physical resolution so that a degenerate
clean variance cannot make the evidence trivially easy to trigger.

\paragraph{Shadow probe.}
The probe must decide whether the GNSS fixes that have returned are consistent with what the platform has
been doing, without letting a persisting spoof pull the state while the decision is taken. A probe that
applied the fixes at partial trust was found on tuning data to drag the state onto a persisting spoof (and the
subsequent inertial coast then continued along the spoof direction), so during PROBE of the position law the
filter evaluates the fix but applies \emph{no} GNSS update and coasts on the inertial and CAI states. For
each fix it forms the joint 6-D innovation $\nu_s = [\hat p^- - p_{\mathrm{g}};\ \hat v^- - v_{\mathrm{g}}]$
and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NIS}_{s} = \nu_s^{\top}\big(H P^{-} H^{\top} + \mathrm{blkdiag}(\Sigma_p^{\mathrm{fix}}, \Sigma_v^{\mathrm{fix}})\big)^{-1}\nu_s ,
\label{eq:shadownis}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ against the coasting covariance $P^{-}$ with the receiver's reported covariance, without the
$\kappa_R$ inflation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rgnss} and without trust scaling. The $\kappa_R$-inflated NIS is
calibrated for filtering correlated fix error, not for a single-epoch consistency test, and is far too
insensitive for this purpose; Eq.~\eqref{eq:shadownis} is approximately $\chi^2_6$ under nominal conditions.
The probe is accepted when the mean of $\mathrm{NIS}_s$ over the probe does not exceed a bound
$\bar\nu_{\mathrm{grade}}$ and $E_s$ members (iii)--(iv) were silent throughout (the clock jump (ii) vetoes
only the clock law, below); member~(i) is superseded by the shadow statistic during the probe, because consistency with the coasting state is the stronger evidence and it
removes a false fire on the legitimate return of the fix after an attack ends. The default bound is
$\chi^2_{6,\,0.99} = 16.81$. Under a pre-registered rule (fixed before the measurement), the default is replaced
by the $99$th percentile of the \emph{clean} distribution of the mean shadow NIS over the first $10\,\mathrm{s}$
after a forced $60\,\mathrm{s}$ outage, measured on tuning seeds disjoint from those used to tune the rest of the
law ($510$--$529$, $60$ windows per grade), whenever the default gives a clean false-veto rate above $1\%$; this
occurred at the MEMS grade, and the frozen bounds are $\bar\nu_{\mathrm{MEMS}} = 25.07$ and
$\bar\nu_{\mathrm{tactical}} = 6.74$. The calibration uses clean data only and never attack data; an unknown IMU
grade uses the default. A consistency-matched adversary that keeps the offset inside the coasting uncertainty
can pass this test; this is a stated limitation (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Consistency-based reacquisition.}
A first valid fix after an outage longer than $T_{\mathrm{gap}}$ would normally have its position weight capped
at $w_{\mathrm{reacq}}$. The cap is waived when this fix is consistent with the coasting state, i.e.\
$\mathrm{NIS}_s \le \bar\nu_{\mathrm{grade}}$, and $E_s$ members (iii)--(iv) are silent (member~(i) is
quarantined at this epoch by the gap reset, and the clock member is not a position signature); otherwise the cap
stays. The recovery time constant $\tau_r$ is unchanged. The same consistency-matched-adversary limit as for the
probe applies.

\paragraph{Position/clock trust split.}
A single GNSS weight forced a co-located meaconer, which appears as a common-mode delay and therefore as a
clock-bias jump with an undisturbed position innovation, to cost the position solution its updates as well,
although the position fix carried no evidence of manipulation. The frozen engine therefore runs two independent
instances of the three-state law with independent timers and states: the position law drives $w_{\mathrm{pos}}$,
which enters the ESKF GNSS update through Eq.~\eqref{eq:reff} (with the update suspended in the shadow probe), and the
clock law drives $w_{\mathrm{clk}}$, which enters the \texttt{ClockKF}, which free-runs on the oscillator model
whenever $w_{\mathrm{clk}} < w_{\mathrm{excl}}$ and, at zero weight, throughout the clock law's probe.
Evidence is routed by domain (Table~\ref{tab:trustsplit}). The single-antenna signatures and the learned
detector, whose feature vector mixes both domains, drive both laws, because a consistent forged solution
forges the clock too; the position jump drives only the position law, and the clock jump only the clock law.

\paragraph{Quantum and inertial trust.}
The quantum weight is rule-based and uses the same recursion with quantum time constants
($\tau_d = T_c$, $\tau_r = 60\,\mathrm{s}$, $T_{\mathrm{clean}} = 5T_c$ for CAI cycle time $T_c$) but without the
bounded-exclusion state machine. Its evidence is
$p_{\mathrm{q}} = 1 - \mathbb{1}[\text{valid}]\cdot\mathrm{clip}\big((c - 0.1)/0.9,\,0,\,1\big)\cdot\mathbb{1}[\mathrm{NIS}_{\mathrm{q}} \le \chi^2_{\mathrm{dof},0.999}]$,
with $c$ the fringe contrast, together with a two-sided Page CUSUM ($k = 0.5$, $h = 8$) on the
standardised, running-mean-removed CAI innovation. The CUSUM runs only while $w_{\mathrm{pos}} > 0.9$, so that it
anchors on an accelerometer bias that GNSS itself constrains, and sets $p_{\mathrm{q}} = 1$ when it crosses $h$.
During CAI dead time the weight is held. The IMU weight is $w_{\min}$ when a sample is missing or an axis
exceeds the saturation limit ($156.9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, assumed) and $1$ otherwise.

\paragraph{Scope of the law.}
The three-state law with the shadow probe, consistency-based reacquisition and the position/clock split is
used by FedQPNT and by B-cont (which differ only in how the detector is trained). Baseline~A, B-bin and B$'$
keep their earlier, simpler laws, and the CAI-off arm of H3 is FedQPNT with the CAI switched off.
Trust trajectories and the fraction of time in each state are not reported in this version; the trust behaviour is summarised by the alarm fraction, detection latency, recovery time and cycle counts of Section~\ref{sec:results}.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Federated learning}
\label{sec:fl}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{What is shared.}
The only trained object is the detector. Each node uploads a \texttt{ModelUpdate}: the weight delta
$\Delta_i = \theta_i - \theta_g$, absolute running normalisation statistics (mean and standard deviation per
feature), and scalar training metrics; a leakage guard rejects any array whose shape does not match the
detector parameters and any non-scalar metric. The uploaded sample count is self-reported and therefore
untrusted. Trust-law and filter hyperparameters are not learned.

\paragraph{Rounds and local training.}
Rounds are bulk-synchronous in simulation time with period $T_{\mathrm{round}} = 60\,\mathrm{s}$. At each
round boundary a live node trains for $E = 2$ epochs (SGD, learning rate $0.05$, batch $64$) on a replay
buffer of at most $20{,}000$ labelled samples, with an optional FedProx term
$\tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert\theta-\theta_g\rVert^2$ \cite{li2020fedprox}; a node with fewer than $64$ labelled
samples sends a heartbeat instead. A node that has never installed a global model reports the current round as its base round, so its update is fresh
rather than stale (a base round of $-1$ would be discarded by the staleness rule below and no node would ever install). A node installs a received global model at the first GNSS epoch after
the simulated uplink and downlink delays, and its trust state is never reset by a model swap.
Communications are simulated in simulation time: a log-normal delay (median $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$, log-scale
$0.5$) plus a serialisation term at $1\,\mathrm{Mbit/s}$, and a Gilbert--Elliott loss channel
($P_{G\rightarrow B} = 0.02$, $P_{B\rightarrow G} = 0.3$, loss $1\%$ in the good and $90\%$ in the bad
state). The round hyperparameters ($E=2$, learning rate $0.05$, $\mu=0$, $R=10$ rounds) were chosen on
tuning seeds by the sanity check below, identically for all FL methods.

\paragraph{Aggregators.}
FedAvg \cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} aggregates with weights $n_i(1+s_i)^{-1/2}$, where $n_i$ is the self-reported
sample count and $s_i$ the staleness in rounds (a staleness discount in the manner of asynchronous federated optimization \cite{xie2019asynchronous}). Updates with $s_i > 3$ are discarded, and a round is skipped
(logged, never silent) unless at least $\lceil 0.5\,N_{\mathrm{live}}\rceil$ fresh updates arrive. FedProx
aggregates as FedAvg; its proximal term acts only on the client. TRIM-NB-R, which combines norm clipping
\cite{sun2019really} with a coordinate-wise trimmed mean \cite{yin2018byzantine}, proceeds as follows, on the
flattened weight delta:
\begin{align}
\tilde\Delta_i &= \Delta_i \cdot \min\!\Big(1,\ \frac{c\cdot \mathrm{median}_j\lVert\Delta_j\rVert}{\lVert\Delta_i\rVert}\Big),
   && c=2,\ \text{or } c=1 \text{ for a node's first two rounds,} \label{eq:clip}\\
\Delta &= \begin{cases}
   \mathrm{TrimMean}_{\beta=0.2}\big(\{(1+s_i)^{-1/2}\tilde\Delta_i\}_{i\in\mathrm{live}}\big) & N_{\mathrm{live}} \ge 5\\
   \mathrm{Median}\big(\{(1+s_i)^{-1/2}\tilde\Delta_i\}_{i\in\mathrm{live}}\big) & N_{\mathrm{live}} < 5,
   \end{cases} \label{eq:trim}\\
r_i &\leftarrow \rho\,r_i + (1-\rho)\max\!\big(0,\ \cos(\tilde\Delta_i,\Delta)\big), && \rho = 0.8, \label{eq:reputation}\\
\theta_g &\leftarrow \theta_g + \eta_s\Delta,\qquad \eta_s=1. \label{eq:serverstep}
\end{align}
The trimmed mean removes $\lfloor\beta N_{\mathrm{live}}\rfloor$ values from each tail per coordinate.
A node whose reputation stays below $0.2$ for three consecutive rounds is quarantined and excluded for the
next ten rounds. Reputation therefore acts only through quarantine and not as an aggregation weight.
Normalisation statistics are aggregated by coordinate-wise median. The sample count never enters the robust
aggregator, which is exactly the quantity a poisoning node can misreport, and FedAvg's use of it is why it is
the vulnerable reference. The simulated poisoning attacks are sign flip (a factor of $-5$),
Gaussian noise at ten times the median honest update norm, label flip, and ``A Little Is Enough''
\cite{baruch2019alie} using a closed-form $z$ (a simplification of the original bookkeeping).

\paragraph{The $\theta_0$ protocol and sanity check.}
Every FL method and the local-only baselines start from the same $\theta_0$, pretrained offline on a
disjoint pretraining seed range with a \emph{restricted} family set that excludes the family used as the
``novel'' family in the H2 and H4 tests, so that ``the detector has never seen this family'' is true of
$\theta_0$ and of the target node's local data and false only of its peers' data. This requirement was added
after two earlier previews, in which $\theta_0$ was trained on every family and the arms agreed to about five
decimals, were rejected as evidence. Before any H2 or H4 run, an FL sanity check requires FedAvg and
TRIM-NB-R on IID nodes that all see all families to reach at least $0.95\times$ the AUC of the centrally
trained detector on the union of their data; only FL hyperparameters are tuned to meet it, on tuning seeds.
The feature normaliser of $\theta_0$ is taken from the supervised detector dataset (the production normaliser, $20{,}552$
samples) rather than estimated from the small pretraining pool ($1{,}470$ samples), whose too-narrow standard deviations made the
normaliser-referenced physical evidence of Section~\ref{sec:trustlaw} fire on clean nodes; the pretrained weights are unchanged.
Each evaluated detector also logs a parameter hash per arm and per round, and the arms are asserted to
differ after the first install.

\paragraph{Fleet runner.}
A fleet mission spawns one server process and $N$ node processes (\texttt{spawn} context, message queues,
one thread each). Each node is a full Environment$+$Agent pair whose federated client wraps the live
detector object of the agent, so an installed model is used from the next tick. The server aggregates in
node-identifier order rather than arrival order and draws the downlink outcome from its own random stream,
so a repeated FL run is bit-identical (unit-tested). A node's local training dataset comes from oracle-labelled training
missions, generated in the parent process before the node is spawned and passed as plain arrays; the node
process never imports the label-join module (static test). Local-only baselines (B-cont, B-bin) are run as
$N$ independent one-node federations with FedAvg, which is the identity for one node, and lossless,
zero-delay communications, so that they do train locally without any exchange and are not handicapped by
simulated network faults that they would not have.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
Navigation output is evaluated at $10\,\mathrm{Hz}$. From the truth labels, $t_{\mathrm{on}}$ is the first
labelled attack sample, $t_{\mathrm{off}}$ the last plus one epoch, and the phases are
$P_{\mathrm{pre}} = [60\,\mathrm{s}, t_{\mathrm{on}})$ (the alignment transient is excluded),
$P_{\mathrm{att}} = [t_{\mathrm{on}}, t_{\mathrm{off}})$ and $P_{\mathrm{post}} = [t_{\mathrm{off}}, T]$.
With $e_h(t) = \lVert \hat p_{EN}(t) - p_{EN}(t)\rVert_2$:
$\mathrm{RMSE}_h(P) = \sqrt{\mathrm{mean}_{t\in P}\,e_h^2}$, $\mathrm{MAX}_h(P)$ and $\mathrm{P95}_h(P)$, with
analogous three-dimensional and velocity errors. Filter consistency is
$\mathrm{ANEES}_{\mathrm{pos}} = \mathrm{mean}\ \delta p^{\top}P_{pos}^{-1}\delta p/3$ (ideal $1$).
Detection uses the trust engine's binary alarm: $t_{\mathrm{det}}$ is the first time from $t_{\mathrm{on}}$
at which the alarm holds for $T_{\mathrm{sus}} = 1\,\mathrm{s}$, $\mathrm{latency}_{\mathrm{on}} = t_{\mathrm{det}} - t_{\mathrm{on}}$,
and a miss is censored at $t_{\mathrm{off}} - t_{\mathrm{on}}$. $P_D$ is the fraction of attacked runs with a
hit. For gradual spoofs, whose onset is physically undetectable, $\mathrm{latency}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is measured from
$t_{\mathrm{eff}}$, the first time the spoof-induced position offset (truth side) exceeds $3\sigma_{\mathrm{nom}}$.
False alarms are rising alarm edges with no active label within $\pm T_{\mathrm{sus}}$, and
$\mathrm{FAR}$ is their count per clean hour. Detector quality is the ROC-AUC of $p$ against the label
(evaluator only). Trust behaviour is the time to distrust ($w_{\mathrm{gnss}} < 0.5$ after onset), the
recovery time $t_{\mathrm{rec}}$ (the first $\tau$ after $t_{\mathrm{off}}$ from which $e_h < \max(2\,\mathrm{RMSE}_h(P_{\mathrm{pre}}),3\,\mathrm{m})$ for
$10\,\mathrm{s}$), the number $N_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ of trust cycles per hour (a downward crossing of $w=0.5$
followed by an upward crossing of $w=0.9$) and the total variation of $w$ per hour. Because PNT includes
timing, clock-bias and clock-drift errors in nanoseconds give $\mathrm{RMSE}_t$ and $\mathrm{MAX}_t$; these are
secondary except that meaconing scenarios report $\mathrm{RMSE}_t$ alongside the primaries, since meaconing
is chiefly a timing attack. FL metrics are the global-model AUC per round on a fixed labelled evaluation set
(evaluator only), rounds skipped and quarantined-node counts. Ground truth and labels are read only by the
evaluator, after the mission, from the recorded files.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:stats}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
Each scenario--method cell uses $30$ test master seeds, paired across methods by the seed-indexed random
streams so that sensor and attack noise are identical between methods. With $n = 30$ a paired test has
$80\%$ power at $\alpha = 0.05$ (two-sided) for a standardised effect $d_z \approx 0.53$. If a pilot on
seeds $600$--$609$ shows $d_z < 0.5$ on a primary metric, $n$ is raised to
$\lceil (z_{0.975}+z_{0.8})^2/d_z^2\rceil + 2$, decided before any test seed is examined. The unit of analysis
is the per-run value averaged over the nodes of the run, since nodes within a run are not independent.
The primary test is the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on paired per-seed differences
\cite{wilcoxon1945individual}; the paired $t$-test is reported in addition only when a Shapiro--Wilk test on the
differences gives $p > 0.05$. Effect sizes are the Hodges--Lehmann median of paired differences
\cite{hodges1963estimates} with a $95\%$ BCa bootstrap interval ($10{,}000$ resamples, a dedicated
seeded stream) \cite{efron1987better}, the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation, and Cohen's $d_z$.
Multiple methods in one scenario use a Friedman test with Nemenyi post-hoc; binary
outcomes use the exact McNemar test; proportions carry Wilson $95\%$ intervals. Censored latencies (misses)
take the censoring value and are handled by the rank tests. Holm--Bonferroni \cite{holm1979simple}
controls the family-wise error over the confirmatory family $\{\mathrm{H1},\ldots,\mathrm{H4}\}\times\{\text{primary
metrics}\}$ (scenarios where applicable); secondary metrics carry unadjusted $p$-values labelled
exploratory. The implemented family has eight tests (Table~\ref{tab:conf}): H1 on $\mathrm{RMSE}_h(P_{\mathrm{att}})$ and
$\mathrm{latency}_{\mathrm{on}}$ against Baseline~A (S2-medium, MEMS grade); H2 and H4 on $P_D@10\,\mathrm{s}$ and onset latency
against B-cont (abrupt and cold-start fleet experiments); and H3 on $\mathrm{latency}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ with and without the CAI (S6, both grades); Holm's procedure uses a fixed $m=8$.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Seed protocol and code freeze}
\label{sec:seeds}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
Seed ranges are disjoint by purpose and fixed in advance.
\emph{Pretraining} (seeds 400--449) trains $\theta_0$.
\emph{Tuning} (500--599) is used for $\kappa_R$, the trust and filter defaults, supervised detector training
(500--549), Platt calibration (550--574), held-out detector checks (575--599), the FL sanity check, and
smoke and safety-principle checks.
A further disjoint tuning-class range (9500--9699) is used for per-design studies such as the
signature-strength sweep. The pilot for the power rule uses 600--609.
\emph{Test} (10000 and above, in disjoint $30$-seed blocks, each used once; Section~\ref{sec:campaign}) is held out behind a gate: the campaign
runner refuses any test seed unless it is invoked with both a final flag and a gate-cleared flag \emph{and}
a gate file records that the gate is cleared. The protocol requires every campaign or fleet run to record the repository revision and the state of the package tree at
launch and at completion, and a run is valid only if the tree is clean and unchanged throughout; core
changes are made only when no evaluation run is live. Trust-law and filter hyperparameters are frozen with
their configuration hash logged before the test sweep, and all random streams derive hierarchically from
the master seed, node identifier and component name. The frozen $\kappa_R$ is $60$ (single source \texttt{DEFAULT\_KAPPA\_R}); the core for the primary results is tag \texttt{core-freeze-6}.
The tag \texttt{core-freeze-6} resolves to git commit \texttt{ca614b7f8a73}; each run record carries its own configuration hash.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{H2/H4 event-level protocol (pre-registered)}
\label{sec:h2prereg}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
The H2 and H4 tests isolate the learned contribution, not the rule-based physical evidence, which by itself
detects some attack families regardless of the detector. Three quantities are therefore reported
separately: the learned-detector-only AUC (calibrated $p$, rule evidence excluded), the operational-score
AUC, and the detection latency. The novel family is evaluated in a regime where the rule evidence does
not fire, with the family absent from $\theta_0$ and from the target node's data and present in its peers'.
For the abrupt-spoofing family the following was registered \emph{before any valid run}, and is fixed:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Setting.} $\theta_0$ pretrained on seeds 400--449 from clean, gradual-spoofing and jamming missions
  (abrupt spoofing and meaconing excluded); abrupt spoof of severity $0.15$ (a $12\,\mathrm{m}$ jump, chosen
  because the rule evidence does not fire at that severity); $N=5$ nodes, $600\,\mathrm{s}$ missions,
  $60\,\mathrm{s}$ rounds, $10$ rounds; FL hyperparameters as in Section~\ref{sec:fl}; live seeds 500--509
  ($n=10$, raised from $5$ for power) in the original registration; a dated amendment moves the live seeds to 10090--10099 for the final core.
\item \emph{Primary.} Event-level detection: $P_D@10\,\mathrm{s}$, the fraction of attack events for which the
  raw detector score $p$ crosses a threshold $\tau$ within $[t_{\mathrm{on}}, t_{\mathrm{on}}+10\,\mathrm{s}]$.
  $\tau$ is set \emph{per arm} to give a clean false-alarm rate of $1$/h, calibrated on clean missions from
  seeds 580--599 (at least $5\,\mathrm{h}$ per arm, disjoint from the live seeds) with the arm's final
  installed model, which gives an equal-FAR comparison. Onset latency (time to the first crossing, censored at
  $60\,\mathrm{s}$ with the number of censored events stated) is reported with it.
\item \emph{Secondary.} AUC of onset-window positives $[t_{\mathrm{on}}, t_{\mathrm{on}}+N]$ against pre-onset
  negatives only, for $N = 10\,\mathrm{s}$ and $N = 5\,\mathrm{s}$. We disclose that $N=10\,\mathrm{s}$ was
  chosen after inspecting the trace of one tuning seed.
\item \emph{Tertiary (descriptive).} Full-window AUC, with an explanation, and a post-attack ``recovery alarm
  rate'' reported separately and not pooled into the negatives.
\item \emph{Statistics.} Paired Wilcoxon on $P_D@10\,\mathrm{s}$ and latency across the $10$ seeds, with the
  per-seed values reported.
\item \emph{Sampling.} Every metric is computed on the $1\,\mathrm{Hz}$ detector-update epochs (the ticks at
  which the detector is scored), not on the $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$ ticks; an earlier invalid run mixed the two.
\end{itemize}
The experiment is run on \texttt{core-freeze-6}, in which a fleet node installs the global model each round. The outcome, with per-seed values, is in Section~\ref{sec:res-fleet}: the primary metric is at its ceiling in both arms in all ten seeds.

\subsection{Validation protocol}
\label{sec:campaign}

The confirmatory family (eight tests, Holm-corrected with fixed $m=8$), the constants frozen from tuning data and the censoring rules were

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{IMU grade as an evaluation dimension}
\label{sec:grade}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
The value of a defence that excludes GNSS depends on how well the platform coasts, and the CAI's benefit may be
small with MEMS gyros, whose tilt error dominates within about a minute, and larger with
tactical-grade gyros; this is a hypothesis, not a finding. The IMU grade is therefore an explicit, pre-registered evaluation dimension: the
attack scenarios are run with both the industrial MEMS and the tactical grade, with the CAI on and off, and
\emph{both} grades are reported. The default grade is not switched to whichever one favours the method.
A coasting-envelope measurement (a forced GNSS outage of $180\,\mathrm{s}$, maximum horizontal error with the CAI
on and off, per grade) characterises the quantum contribution under denial; the measured values are in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Safety principle}
\label{sec:safety}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
A defended method must never be substantially worse than the undefended reference across the stated threat
envelope. This is pre-registered as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMSE}_h^{\mathrm{def}}(P_{\mathrm{att}}) \le \mathrm{RMSE}_h^{\mathrm{undef}}(P_{\mathrm{att}}) + 3\sigma_{\mathrm{nom}},
\label{eq:safety}
\end{equation}
for every defended method and every attack scenario, where $\sigma_{\mathrm{nom}}$ is the across-seed standard
deviation of the undefended method's nominal-scenario $\mathrm{RMSE}_h$, measured once and identically for
all methods. It is a noise floor, not the spread of attack-phase errors. Detection of a drifting spoofer
depends on the strength $s$ of its single-antenna C/N0 signature (the shared common-mode fluctuation and its
convergence, scaled from $s=0$ to $s=1$). The paper therefore states a minimum signature strength,
$s \gtrsim 0.5$, as an explicit operating assumption, and registers a measurement of the $s < 0.5$ failure boundary, with a harm
analysis, over signature strengths $s\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1\}$. Failures of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:safety} are reported as measured and are not tuned away. A relaxed criterion may be adopted
only if pre-registered prospectively, before the test sweep, and labelled as defined after the tuning data.
That sweep was not executed in the round-3 test campaign, so the failure boundary remains a tuning-seed diagnostic. The round-3 outcome of Eq.~\eqref{eq:safety} for the scenarios that were run, and the recovery-after-jamming requirement (jamming must not slow recovery), are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

%=====================================================================
\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
%=====================================================================
All results in this section come from a single pre-registered round on the code at tag \texttt{core-freeze-6}: test seeds $10060$--$10089$ ($n=30$ per scenario--method cell; $5{,}340$ of $5{,}340$ runs completed) and, for the H2/H4 fleet experiment, seeds $10090$--$10099$ ($n=10$). The nominal noise floor of the safety rule is $\sigma_{\mathrm{nom}} = 1.098\,\mathrm{m}$ (industrial MEMS) and $1.117\,\mathrm{m}$ (tactical), so $3\sigma_{\mathrm{nom}} = 3.29$ and $3.35\,\mathrm{m}$. Cells report the median over seeds unless stated. The alarm fraction is the fraction of epochs outside the attack label in which the trust alarm is active, so it includes the post-attack exclusion dwell. In the single-node scenarios the FedQPNT arm is the node configuration with the frozen supervised detector and no federation rounds (a single node has no peers), so single-node comparisons test the continuous trust law and the quantum-aided filter; federated learning enters only in the fleet experiments (S5, S8, S9, S12, S15, H2, H4). Verdicts are paired Hodges--Lehmann (HL) differences with $95\%$ BCa intervals; ``better''/``worse'' means that the interval excludes zero, and these descriptive verdicts are not part of the Holm family. Both IMU grades are always reported.

\subsection{Confirmatory family}
Table~\ref{tab:conf} lists the eight pre-registered tests. \subsection{Headline comparison}
Tables~\ref{tab:head1} and~\ref{tab:head2} compare FedQPNT (FQ) with the undefended filter (UD) and Baseline~A (BA). The pattern separates cleanly into scenarios in which FedQPNT costs nothing or helps, and scenarios in which it is worse.

\emph{Nominal operation and jamming (S1, S3).} FedQPNT is equal to the undefended filter in nominal horizontal error at the MEMS grade ($2.91$ versus $2.90\,\mathrm{m}$) and marginally better at the tactical grade (HL $-0.036\,\mathrm{m}$, CI $[-0.072,-0.006]$); the alarm fraction is $0$ in all arms. Its clock error is marginally worse ($10.4$ versus $10.0\,\mathrm{ns}$, HL $+0.28\,\mathrm{ns}$, CI $[0.14, 0.43]$). The S1 acceptance criteria pass on horizontal error relative to fixed trust (median ratio $0.997$ MEMS, $0.991$ tactical; bound $1.05$), false alarms ($0$/h), position ANEES ($0.84$ and $0.94$, band $[0.5, 2]$) and mean GNSS weight ($0.975$ at both grades), but the total variation of $w_{\mathrm{gnss}}$ ($12.5$ and $12.6$ per hour against a bound of $1$) fails. Under jamming (S3) attack-window error is equal to the undefended and Baseline~A arms at both grades, and post-attack error is marginally lower (HL $-0.042\,\mathrm{m}$ MEMS, $-0.029\,\mathrm{m}$ tactical).

\emph{Displaced meaconer (S2-DM).} At the MEMS grade the attack-window horizontal error is lower than undefended (median $943$ versus $1{,}340\,\mathrm{m}$; HL $-471\,\mathrm{m}$, CI $[-743,-202]$) and than Baseline~A ($2{,}150\,\mathrm{m}$; HL $-5{,}020\,\mathrm{m}$, CI $[-8{,}620,-881]$). At the tactical grade it is indistinguishable from undefended (HL $-239\,\mathrm{m}$, CI $[-508, 17.9]$) and lower than Baseline~A ($3{,}010\,\mathrm{m}$; HL $-2{,}910\,\mathrm{m}$, CI $[-3{,}940,-1{,}090]$). Clock error falls from $5{,}220\,\mathrm{ns}$ undefended to $634\,\mathrm{ns}$ (HL $-4{,}570\,\mathrm{ns}$, CI $[-5{,}180,-4{,}180]$), and is also lower than Baseline~A ($1{,}220$ and $2{,}230\,\mathrm{ns}$ at the two grades; HL $-1{,}390$ and $-1{,}860\,\mathrm{ns}$). The cost is paid after the attack: post-attack horizontal error is higher than both comparators ($1{,}310$ versus $859$ undefended and $268\,\mathrm{m}$ Baseline~A at the MEMS grade), and the alarm fraction is $0.169$ versus $0.021$ undefended and $0.0027$ Baseline~A.

\emph{Gradual spoofing (S2-low/medium/high).} Here FedQPNT is worse than the undefended filter in the attack window at both grades (medium severity, MEMS: $760$ versus $254\,\mathrm{m}$; tactical: $973$ versus $254\,\mathrm{m}$; low severity: $766$ versus $127\,\mathrm{m}$ at MEMS; high: $734$ versus $381\,\mathrm{m}$) and equal to Baseline~A at the MEMS grade for all three severities (HL CIs include zero, e.g.\ $+85.3\,\mathrm{m}$ at medium). Post-attack error is much larger than for either comparator (medium, MEMS: $1{,}120$ versus $189\,\mathrm{m}$ undefended and $5.69\,\mathrm{m}$ Baseline~A), and the alarm fraction is $0.164$ versus $0$. Detection is nevertheless faster than both comparators by $0.5$--$1\,\mathrm{s}$ at all severities and grades.

\emph{Jam-then-spoof (S4) and the four-hour drift spoof (S6).} In S4 (a $40\,\mathrm{s}$ jam followed by a drift spoof) FedQPNT detects the attack at a median $42\,\mathrm{s}$ against $61\,\mathrm{s}$ for both comparators (HL $-19.5\,\mathrm{s}$, CI $[-20.5,-19.5]$), but its attack-window error ($461$ and $638\,\mathrm{m}$) exceeds the undefended filter ($137\,\mathrm{m}$) at both grades, and Baseline~A ($227$ and $104\,\mathrm{m}$) as well. In S6 the attack-window error is $16{,}200\,\mathrm{m}$ (MEMS) and $2{,}680\,\mathrm{m}$ (tactical) against $438\,\mathrm{m}$ undefended; the alarm fraction is $0.132$ and the clock error $3{,}790$ versus $11.6\,\mathrm{ns}$ undefended. FedQPNT detects the spoof as quickly as the undefended filter does or faster ($1\,\mathrm{s}$ median; HL $-0.5\,\mathrm{s}$, CI $[-1,-1]$), but in this scenario detection does not translate into lower error.

\subsection{Trust chattering and long-duration stability}
Across the ten S7 variants (toggle periods $p\in\{2,5,10,20,60\}\,\mathrm{s}$, both grades) the maximum number of trust cycles per hour observed in any run was $4$ for $p\le 5\,\mathrm{s}$, $1$ for $p=10$ and $20\,\mathrm{s}$, and $29$ (MEMS) and $13$ (tactical) for $p=60\,\mathrm{s}$, all within the pre-registered bound of $223$ per hour (and the $133$ per hour regular-grid annotation). The bound is the timing-free result of Section~\ref{sec:trustlaw} (every cycle lasts at least $16.1\,\mathrm{s}$). Bounded chattering does not imply small error: FedQPNT is worse than undefended on attack-window horizontal error in all S7 variants, and on the tactical grade rapid toggling accumulates long exclusion, with median attack-window error of $106$ and $312\,\mathrm{m}$ at $p=10$ and $20\,\mathrm{s}$ and a mean maximum-error excess of up to $6.6\times 10^{4}\,\mathrm{m}$ at $p=60\,\mathrm{s}$ (Table~\ref{tab:safety}); the stated operating envelope (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) notes that a pure blackout gives the same result.

In the four-hour nominal mission (S14) the median horizontal RMSE of the last hour is $0.786$ (MEMS) and $0.840$ (tactical) of the first hour (criterion $\le 1.2$), i.e.\ there is no slow drift; the median RMSE$_h$ is $2.98\,\mathrm{m}$ [IQR $2.71$--$3.14$] and $2.90\,\mathrm{m}$ [$2.71$--$3.07$], the alarm fraction is $0$, every run is finite and positive-definite, and the false-alarm rate is $0.067$ per hour (criterion $\le 1$). The registry marks S14 ``not evaluable'' overall because its covariance-growth leg is not emitted by the runner.

\subsection{Quantum aiding (S6-coast, H3)}
In the exploratory $180\,\mathrm{s}$ forced-outage test the CAI did not reduce the maximum coasting error. The median ratio of the maximum error without and with the CAI is $1.006$ at the MEMS grade (mean maximum error $513.4\,\mathrm{m}$ without and $521.4\,\mathrm{m}$ with the CAI; HL $-0.34\,\mathrm{m}$, CI $[-11.7, 20.9]$) and $0.986$ at the tactical grade ($106.1$ versus $104.6\,\mathrm{m}$; HL $+0.035\,\mathrm{m}$, CI $[-4.77, 2.62]$); the $2.1$--$3.4\times$ envelope seen on tuning seeds was not reproduced (Fig.~\ref{fig:cai}). Pre-attack horizontal error with the CAI is marginally larger (HL $+0.031\,\mathrm{m}$ MEMS, $+0.071\,\mathrm{m}$ tactical, CIs exclude zero). The confirmatory H3 test shows identical $\mathrm{latency}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ with and without the CAI at both grades (Table~\ref{tab:conf}).

\subsection{Fleet experiments and federated learning}
\label{sec:res-fleet}
\emph{Functional federation.} Fleet nodes install the global model: in the abrupt H2 experiment FedQPNT nodes installed it in $9.6$ of $10$ rounds on average (range $8$--$10$ over the ten seeds), against $8.0$ ($2$--$10$) for B-cont. In S5 (node failures and delayed updates) no run deadlocked or aborted ($P=1.000$) and $5.93$ rounds per run were gracefully skipped; in S8 a cold-start node received the global model within two rounds of joining in every run ($P=1.000$). In S15 (simultaneous attack on $30\%$ of a ten-node fleet) the detection probability over attacked nodes is $1.000$, the attacked-node attack-window error is $935\pm115\,\mathrm{m}$ (mean, $95\%$ CI), the mean false-alarm rate on unattacked nodes is $0$ per hour, and no quarantine event occurred in any run ($P=1.000$). The clean-window horizontal error and alarm rate in S12 (sign-flip poisoning at $f=20\%$ and $40\%$) are identical to the clean-fleet arm ($2.79\pm0.14\,\mathrm{m}$, $0$ false alarms per hour). The AUC-based legs of S5, S9 and S12 are undefined because those scenarios contain no attack (the registry records them as failed or not evaluable with an undefined value), so the poisoning-robustness of TRIM-NB-R against FedAvg is \emph{not} evaluated in this round.

\emph{H2 and H4 (abrupt spoof, $n=10$).} At the pre-registered per-arm threshold both FedQPNT and B-cont detect every event at the onset epoch ($P_D@10\,\mathrm{s}=1$, latency $0\,\mathrm{s}$, $0$ censored events, in both the H2 and the cold-start H4 experiments), so the paired tests are degenerate ($p=1$). The calibrated thresholds are $\tau = 0.0116$ (FedQPNT) and $0.0090$ (B-cont) in H2. The secondary onset-window AUC ($N=10\,\mathrm{s}$) is near chance for both arms: H2, $0.505$ versus $0.507$ (paired $p=0.625$, uncorrected); H4, $0.526$ versus $0.501$ (paired $p=0.006$, uncorrected, exploratory, not in the Holm family). Per-seed values range from $0.20$ to $0.89$ in both arms (Fig.~\ref{fig:h2h4}) and the H4 mean difference of $+0.025$ is small against that spread; we do not interpret it as a detection benefit. Under the control drift the AUC is high for both arms ($0.958$ versus $0.958$).

\subsection{Other scenarios}
For the sample-rate-mismatch scenarios (S10) the position ANEES lies in the band $[0.5,2]$ at GNSS rates of $1$ and $2\,\mathrm{Hz}$ ($0.65$--$0.72$ and $1.35$--$1.47$) and outside it at $5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ ($3.26$--$3.47$) and $10\,\mathrm{Hz}$ ($6.40$--$6.62$), independent of the assumed CAI cycle time. \section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Discussion}
\paragraph{When trust-based exclusion pays.} The measured benefits are concentrated where excluding GNSS removes a large, sustained error. Against the displaced meaconer, which drags the position solution towards the attacker's antenna and corrupts the clock, FedQPNT lowers attack-window horizontal error relative to Baseline~A at both IMU grades and relative to the undefended filter at the MEMS grade, and reduces median clock error about eight-fold ($634$ versus $5{,}220\,\mathrm{ns}$): timing is where exclusion helps most and most consistently. It also detects attack onset earlier than a binary detect-and-exclude baseline (by $0.5$ to $1\,\mathrm{s}$ in the gradual-spoof scenarios and by $19.5\,\mathrm{s}$ in jam-then-spoof), and the confirmatory H1 latency test is the only test of the family that rejects. These gains cost nothing in nominal operation or under jamming (S1, S3: equal to the undefended filter, no false alarms), the trust state machine cycles at most $29$ times per hour against a proven bound of $223$, and a four-hour mission shows no drift. The design therefore behaves as intended on the axes of detection speed, timing protection and benign-case safety.

\paragraph{When it does not.} Against ordinary drifting spoofs (S2) and the jam-then-spoof, long-drift and toggling scenarios (S4, S6, S7), FedQPNT's horizontal error is larger than that of the undefended filter, and for S2 and S4 usually no better than Baseline~A, so the safety principle fails in all of them. Two mechanisms stated in the pre-registered operating envelope are consistent with this pattern. First, when the spoof offset is small, the inertial coast drifts further than the spoof displaces the position: at the MEMS grade, the attack-window error of the undefended filter that simply follows the spoof ($127$ to $381\,\mathrm{m}$ across S2 severities) is below the coast error of an excluding filter ($730$ to $770\,\mathrm{m}$), so exclusion cannot win on a short horizon. Second, post-attack error is dominated by the designed $60\,\mathrm{s}$ re-admission dwell during which GNSS is not used; the post-attack error of FedQPNT ($1{,}100$ to $1{,}300\,\mathrm{m}$ in S2) is far above both comparators, and the alarm fraction of $0.164$ is consistent with this dwell. The tactical grade should coast better, yet its S2 and S4 errors are not lower than the MEMS ones, so the MEMS coast floor alone does not explain the loss; a plausible but untested contributor is the residual over-confidence of the attitude and accelerometer-bias states during long coasts (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). We therefore read the results as characterising the regime in which exclusion pays (large or timing-corrupting offsets) rather than as a uniform improvement over not defending.

\paragraph{CAI and federated learning showed no measurable effect here.} The CAI did not reduce coasting error (ratio $1.006$ and $0.986$; CIs include zero) or the effective detection latency, and federated learning did not change detection in the abrupt-spoof experiments (identical ceiling detection, onset AUC near $0.5$). We offer three hypotheses, none tested here. (i) The rigid-mode CAI loses contrast above $\Omega_c\approx17\,\mathrm{mrad/s}$, so its aiding is intermittent in manoeuvring motion, and in the simulated platform its bias observation may add little to an inertial filter whose coast error is dominated by tilt (attitude) error. (ii) The rule-based physical evidence, not the learned detector, may drive detection at the tested severities, which would make detection latency insensitive to both the CAI and the federated model; the $P_D@10\,\mathrm{s}$ metric is at its ceiling at the registered threshold, so a harder setting (a lower-severity or signature-free family, a stricter latency metric) is needed to separate the arms. (iii) The experiments are simulation-only and use ten live seeds for the fleet tests. The null results are therefore results about this configuration; they are not evidence that quantum aiding or fleet learning cannot help.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The following limitations are already established by the project's own validation work and the decision log
and are stated here rather than deferred, in keeping with the scientific-integrity requirement that known
weaknesses be disclosed regardless of how they affect the headline comparison. Where a limitation has a
measured extent on the test seeds, the number is in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FedQPNT does not reduce position error under ordinary spoofing.} In the gradual-spoof scenarios (S2-low, medium, high), jam-then-spoof (S4), the long drift spoof (S6) and the toggling scenarios (S7), the attack-window horizontal error is larger than that of the undefended filter at both IMU grades, usually no better than Baseline~A, and the safety principle fails; post-attack error is increased by the $60\,\mathrm{s}$ re-admission dwell. The benefits reported are confined to detection latency, timing, the displaced meaconer, and absence of nominal cost.
\item \textbf{No benefit from quantum aiding or federated learning was measured.} The CAI did not reduce coasting error or $\mathrm{latency}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (H3 null), and federated learning did not change abrupt-spoof detection (H2, H4 null: ceiling detection, onset AUC near $0.5$). The single-node results involve no federation, the fleet tests use $n=10$ seeds, and the poisoning-robustness of TRIM-NB-R against FedAvg was not evaluated because the AUC legs of S5, S9 and S12 are undefined. The explanations in Section~\ref{sec:discussion} are hypotheses.
\item \textbf{Detection has a signature-strength envelope.} Detection of a drifting single-antenna spoofer
  relies on the strength $s$ of its C/N0 signature (Section~\ref{sec:safety}). The detector is trained at
  full strength and detects reliably at $s \gtrsim 0.5$; for $s < 0.5$ it fails, its ranking of spoofed
  against clean epochs falling to chance and, at $s = 0$, inverting. The minimum strength $s \gtrsim 0.5$ is
  therefore an explicit operating assumption, not a guarantee, and a fully signature-free spoofer
  (multi-antenna, per-satellite-power-matched) is outside the threat model. Against such a spoofer the
  probe-based recovery has no physical signature to rely on. The round-3 campaign did not run the signature-strength sweep.
\item \textbf{A consistency-matched adversary can pass the shadow probe.} The probe accepts GNSS whose
  innovation against the coasting state is within a calibrated bound (Section~\ref{sec:trustlaw}). An adversary
  that keeps its offset inside the coasting uncertainty is by construction consistent with the coasting state
  and can pass. The bound is calibrated on clean data per IMU grade and, at the MEMS grade, is loose (a long
  coast has a large covariance), so spoofs below roughly the coast uncertainty can be admitted at that grade,
  whereas the tighter tactical-grade bound rejects far smaller offsets. Once admitted, the damage per epoch is
  bounded by the coasting covariance, not unbounded. The detection-power figures that motivated the calibration
  were obtained on tuning seeds and are diagnostics, not findings. The same limit applies to the
  consistency-based waiver of the reacquisition cap. The detection power of the probe against offset spoofs was not measured separately on the test seeds.
\item \textbf{Clock-only evidence is weak just after a long outage.} After a long GNSS outage the receiver
  oscillator's own uncertainty is large (a TCXO drifts by about $263\,\mathrm{m}$, one standard deviation, over
  $180\,\mathrm{s}$), so the clock-jump evidence of Eq.~\eqref{eq:clkjump}, which is normalised by that
  uncertainty to avoid false alarms on ordinary holdover drift, detects a replay-delay step only in proportion
  to its size: a $750\,\mathrm{m}$ step is only about $2.9\sigma$ against the $5\sigma$ threshold. Immediately
  after a long outage, meaconing therefore has to be caught by the other evidence (position, C/N0, detector).
\item \textbf{The filter may remain over-confident in attitude and accelerometer bias.} $\kappa_R$ is a surrogate
  for the time-correlated error of a loosely coupled fix. The receiver's reported velocity covariance was
  checked and found honest, and $\kappa_R = 60$ brings the position/velocity consistency to nominal, but the
  attitude and bias states were over-confident when last measured against the effective-bias truth, which includes turn-on bias,
  GM1, random walk and scale-factor/misalignment aliasing (accelerometer-bias NEES about six times
  nominal on tuning seeds, before the covariance corrections of Section~\ref{sec:fusion}). The round-3 campaign reports position ANEES only ($0.84$ and $0.94$ in S1) and did not re-measure these states on \texttt{core-freeze-6}.
  The residual is a stated limitation. Results that depend on attitude or
  bias estimates (coasting envelope, the CAI contribution, multi-hour stability) carry this caveat, and the
  test-seed gate that guards them is described in Section~\ref{sec:seeds}; the gated scenarios were run once on the frozen core and are reported as measured.
\item \textbf{The evaluation is simulation-only.} The CAI noise density, per-interrogation-time scaling and
  bursty-outlier fault process are fitted to real cold-atom interferometer laboratory shot data
  \cite{darmagnac2024atom}, but the vehicle/UAV trajectories, the GNSS constellation and signal propagation
  (modelled at the raw-observable level, not the signal or IF-sample level), the receiver oscillator, and the
  resulting sensor time series are all simulated. The simulated truth clock uses the same TCXO model as the
  filter, so the timing results are model-consistent by construction. No public dataset places a moving quantum
  inertial sensor under GNSS attack, so a fully real-data closed-loop evaluation is not currently possible, and
  receiver-internal (tracking-loop, correlator) effects are not captured.
\item \textbf{The modelled meaconer is co-located.} The meaconing attack applies a common-mode delay to all
  satellites, which is nearly degenerate with a receiver clock bias and barely disturbs the position solution.
  The position/clock trust split can therefore be evaluated only on this co-located threat. A displaced meaconer
  pins the position toward the meaconer's antenna and is a distinct threat; a displaced variant has been added
  as a separate scenario entry and is held out of detector training, so that it tests generalisation.
  The displaced variant (S2-DM) was run in round 3: it lowers attack-window error relative to Baseline~A and, at the MEMS grade, relative to the undefended filter, and cuts clock error, but raises post-attack error and the alarm fraction (Section~\ref{sec:results}); the co-located case was not part of the round-3 matrix. Even for the co-located case, the learned detector drives both laws
  and may still depress the position weight, so the split's benefit is measured, not assumed.
\item \textbf{Several scenario legs are narrowed relative to the pre-registered intent.} A cold-atom
  \emph{fault injector} was not built, so the leg in which a CAI bias drift drives $w_{\mathrm{q}}$ below $0.5$
  within ten cycles is not exercised (S6 is instead the H3 scenario, with the CAI on and off); S7 does not vary the
  detector noise near its decision threshold and toggles abrupt-spoof segments at $50\%$ duty; S10 does not
  vary CAI cycle-time jitter; S12 in the campaign matrix covers only a subset of the poisoning types, the others
  being covered by a separate script; and S15 does not include the attacked-node damage legs. These narrowings
  were decided before any test-seed data.
\item \textbf{FedProx is not evaluated.} It is implemented on the client side, but with the frozen $\mu = 0$ it
  equals FedAvg and no fleet arm exists for it, so it is dropped from the evaluated methods and from the claims.
\item \textbf{Rigid-mode quantum aiding is intermittent during ordinary manoeuvres.} The measured
  rigid-mode contrast roll-off rate implies a rotation-rate tolerance of only
  $\Omega_c \approx 17\,\mathrm{mrad/s}$ (field grade), well below the rotation rates of an ordinary
  vehicle turn ($\sim 0.3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$). The CAI therefore loses contrast, and its aiding becomes
  intermittent, during manoeuvring rather than only during straight-and-level segments. This is reported
  as a real effect, not corrected for, and it is expected to weaken the measured quantum benefit relative
  to an idealized always-on quantum sensor.
\item \textbf{The novelty claimed here is scoped, not absolute.} G\textsuperscript{2}FL
  \cite{liu2026g2fl} already provides robust federated learning for GNSS spoofing detection under
  Byzantine clients. FedQPNT's contribution is the closed loop that couples an FL-trained detector to a
  continuous, evidence-bounded trust law and to physical quantum-sensor aiding, not the robust-aggregation
  idea in isolation.
\item \textbf{Hypotheses are pre-registered.} H1--H4 are reported whatever their outcome, including a null or reversed
  result, per the project's own scientific-integrity rule.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Stated operating envelope.} Properties of the frozen design and scenario set, stated before the primary round and not changed for it:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{MEMS coast floor.} At the MEMS grade, free-inertial drift can exceed a small spoof offset, so exclusion cannot beat following that spoof during the attack.
\item \textbf{Exclusion dwell.} Post-attack error is dominated by the designed $T_{\mathrm{ex}} = 60\,\mathrm{s}$ dwell before re-admission.
\item \textbf{S7 chatter on tactical.} Rapid toggling can cause long cumulative exclusion; the coast error then equals that of a pure blackout.
\item \textbf{S11 lockout.} If true GNSS noise is at least twice the reported value, the filter can lock out GNSS; no noise-scale estimation is added.
\item \textbf{S5/S12 AUC.} Detector AUC is not evaluable there (no attack in those scenarios).
\item \textbf{H2/H4 primary.} The event-level primary metric is degenerate (ceiling) in the setting used: in round 3 both arms detect every event at the onset epoch ($P_D@10\,\mathrm{s}=1$, latency $0\,\mathrm{s}$, ten seeds), so the primary tests cannot discriminate.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%=====================================================================
We have presented FedQPNT, a system architecture that closes the loop between a learned, fleet-shared
attack detector, a continuous and evidence-bounded per-sensor trust law, and physical quantum inertial
aiding whose noise and fault behaviour is calibrated against real laboratory data, and we have evaluated it
in a pre-registered round of $5{,}340$ simulated runs on unseen seeds. The evidence supports a specific and
bounded set of conclusions. The continuous trust law detects attack onset significantly earlier than a
detect-and-exclude baseline (median $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$, Holm-adjusted $p=0.004$), adds no measurable cost in nominal
operation or under jamming, cycles trust at most $29$ times per hour against a proven bound of $223$, remains stable over
four hours, and, against a displaced meaconer, lowers attack-window horizontal error relative to the baseline and cuts median
clock error about eight-fold. The study did not show that the cold-atom accelerometer improves coasting
or detection (coasting-error ratio $1.006$ and $0.986$; H3 null), that federated learning improves abrupt-spoof
detection (H2 and H4 null, with the primary metric at its ceiling), or that FedQPNT reduces position error under
ordinary drifting spoofs, where its error exceeded that of the undefended filter at both IMU grades and the
safety principle failed; the position-error component of H1 was not significant. These outcomes delimit the regime in which GNSS exclusion pays, namely large or
timing-corrupting offsets, and point to future work on harder detection settings that can separate the arms, on filter consistency during long
coasts, and on the re-admission dwell. The limitations that most affect these conclusions are the
simulation-only evaluation, the intermittency of rigid-mode quantum aiding above ordinary vehicle turn rates, and the
GNSS covariance inflation required by the loosely-coupled filter.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
Every numeric default in the system specification is tagged as literature-derived, design-derived, or an
explicit assumption requiring a sensitivity sweep before publication (project decision log, D-001 and
architecture specification conventions). All Monte Carlo experiments use a hierarchical, deterministic
random-number-generator seeding scheme (one independent stream per node, component and purpose) so that
adding or removing a component does not change the noise realization seen by any other component, and so
that paired statistical tests across methods are exact rather than approximate. Pre-training, tuning and
test seed ranges are disjoint and fixed before any test-seed result is examined; the trust-law and filter
hyperparameters are frozen, with their configuration hash logged, before the test-seed sweep begins. The
quantum-sensor calibration pipeline, from the raw Zenodo-hosted dataset \cite{darmagnac2024dataset}
through per-interrogation-time robust noise statistics to the bursty-outlier channel, is scripted
end-to-end and is re-run by an automated test suite. No experimental result, ablation, or scenario is
withheld or dropped after being observed; any exclusion, if one becomes necessary, is logged with its
reason in the project decision log. Code, configuration hashes, and the full 15-scenario result set will
be released alongside the camera-ready version.
